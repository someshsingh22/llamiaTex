We evaluate \textsc{LLAMIA} on \textsc{LLAMIA-Bench}, a suite of four tasks spanning three collaboration facets: behavioral imitation, state assessment, and natural-language explanation (\cref{sec:app_bench}). Tasks are chosen to be unsolvable by either component alone, ensuring that performance reflects genuine LLM--agent collaboration rather than isolated backbone or engine capability. For comparing LLAMIA with cosed source models where training isnt possible, we pair them with Lc0 as a verbalized tool, testing whether model scale alone can compensate for the absence of latent integration. To evaluate against open-source models we create three settings: an untrained tool-use baseline, a 
training-matched verbalization system, and our internalized model, isolating the integration interface as the sole variable.  Within the 
open-source trained systems, we report both SFT and SFT + DAPO checkpoints to disentangle the contributions of supervised pretraining and reinforcement 
learning.

\subsection{LLAMIA-Bench}
\textsc{LLAMIA-Bench} spans four tasks drawn from prominent problems in chess literature and industry, each unsolvable by either component alone: the subagent produces no language, while the LLM lacks the positional signals that make chess-specific judgments possible. The suite covers behavior cloning, puzzle understanding, move annotation, and game-level commentary (details in \cref{sec:app_bench}).
  Behavior cloning, difficulty estimation, and move annotation are drawn from established benchmarks~\cite{mcilroy2020aligning,lichess2024puzzles,jhamtani-etal-2018-learning}. We introduce three new evaluation targets: \textbf{Wild BC}, three OOD
  splits (GM-25, Low-Time, $\Delta$Elo) probing generalization to grandmaster play, time pressure, and asymmetric skill-gap
  ; \textbf{interest estimation}, ranking puzzles by community-derived interestingness scores, a signal with no
   verbal proxy in any engine output; and \textbf{Agadmator-2K}, the first large-scale game-level commentary dataset of 1,900 narrated games with move-aligned
  transcripts.
  Full dataset descriptions, metrics, and per-task prompts appear in Appendices~\ref{sec:app_bench}--\ref{sec:app_prompts}.

\paragraph{Baselines}
We compare four systems. \textbf{(1) GPT-5.1\,+\,Lc0}: the strongest frontier model with verbalized Lc0-BT4 tool access. \textbf{(2) Qwen3-14B\,+\,Lc0}: the LLAMIA backbone with the same verbalized tool and 5-shot prompting, without training. \textbf{(3) LLAMIA-Verb}: the matched-recipe ablation with the latent channel replaced by the verbalized tool, isolating the integration interface as the sole variable. \textbf{(4) LLAMIA}: our full system with latent state-token integration. Extended comparisons including LLM-only baselines, SFT and DAPO checkpoints at all three model sizes(4B,8B and 14B), frontier model comparisons, and task-specific experts are in \cref{sec:app_results}. Training details and hyperparameters are in Appendix~\ref{sec:app_implementation}.

\paragraph{Metrics}
Each task has a primary metric detailed in \cref{sec:app_bench}: move-matching accuracy (behavior cloning), Spearman $\rho$ (difficulty and interest), and G-eval and BLEU-2 (annotation and commentary). Primary metrics include 95\% bootstrap confidence intervals where sample sizes warrant; per-task breakdowns with CIs appear in \cref{sec:app_results}.

\subsection{Verbalization Debt}
\label{sec:results_vd}
\somesh{Now that we have quantitatively differentiated LLAMIA on all tasks against Frontier + Task specific Finetunes we discuss the verbalization debt through table 1 and figure again, divide into key insights, Verbalization gap is consistent across scale, throughout training, }

We define \textbf{verbalization debt} as the performance gap between LLAMIA and LLAMIA-Verb when backbone, subagent, and training recipe are held fixed. The $\Delta$ row of \cref{tab:main_bench} reports this quantity per task. Internalization improves every task, but the margin spans an order of magnitude. At one extreme, the engine's policy shape and value gradients that distinguish engaging puzzles from routine ones have no text equivalent; every verbalized system scores ${\leq}12$ on Interest regardless of model scale (\cref{fig:combined}c), while LLAMIA reaches $52$. At the other, the engine's top-$k$ moves already cover most human play, leaving a modest gap on in-distribution behavior cloning. The per-task analysis in \cref{sec:app_landscape} traces each task's debt to the specific information that verbalization discards.

\paragraph{The debt persists throughout training.}
The gap between LLAMIA and LLAMIA-Verb widens throughout DAPO, not just at convergence (\cref{fig:combined}a). Per-task curves (\cref{fig:combined}b) show where the verbal channel saturates: LLAMIA-Verb gains partial signal on behavior cloning and difficulty, where the verbalized output carries a useful proxy (top-$k$ moves, solution length), but stays near-flat on interest and commentary, where no such proxy exists. Scaling the backbone from 4B to 14B does not close the gap. The bottleneck is the interface, not model capacity.

\paragraph{RL compounds the advantage of latent access.}
The gain from DAPO over SFT is consistently larger under latent access than under verbalization (\cref{sec:results_ablations}). On commentary, latent SFT alone surpasses verbal DAPO; adding RL doubles the gain again. The same pattern holds on annotation. RL discovers multi-step collaboration strategies (counterfactual queries, lookahead chains) that produce useful gradient signal only when the return channel carries the agent's full state, not a text summary (\cref{sec:results_agency}).

We classify collaboration episodes into five strategies (identified by a GPT-4o judge, $\kappa{=}0.78$ vs.\ human raters; definitions in \cref{fig:agency_heatmaps}): \emph{engine-follow}, \emph{consult-then-override}, \emph{counterfactual query}, \emph{multi-step lookahead}, and \emph{abstention}. LLAMIA selects different strategies for different tasks: engine-follow dominates gameplay (65\%), consult-then-override dominates behavior cloning (48\%), and counterfactual query dominates commentary (40\%). LLAMIA-Verb collapses to engine-follow on every task (62--76\%), regardless of what the task requires (\cref{fig:agency_heatmaps}). \cref{fig:agency_diversity} traces how this gap emerges during training. Both systems develop similar invocation patterns through ${\sim}30\%$ of DAPO steps; after ${\sim}40\%$, the internalized model begins exploiting counterfactual-query and multi-step-lookahead patterns that require reading the agent's full state, not just its top recommendation. The verbalized model has learned one way to use the engine; the internalized model has learned several.

\subsection{Ablations}
\label{sec:results_ablations}
% =========================================================================

We ablate three pipeline components; full tables appear in \cref{sec:app_ablations}.

\paragraph{SFT vs.\ DAPO.}
Latent SFT alone matches or exceeds verbal DAPO on four of six tasks (e.g., Commentary: $0.58$ vs.\ $0.40$ G-eval). DAPO adds the most where multi-step collaboration is required (Commentary: $+0.17$ G-eval; Annotation Comparative: $+10.3$\,pp) and the least on single-step prediction (BC: $+2$\,pp). The SFT-to-DAPO gain is consistently larger under latent access than under verbalization, consistent with RL's advantage concentrating on collaboration sequences that are productive only when the return channel carries the full agent state.

\paragraph{Agent architecture and strength.}
We replace Lc0-BT4 with five weaker networks spanning 2000--2500 Elo across two architecture families (SE-ResNet, Transformer). Performance increases monotonically with agent strength on all tasks (\cref{tab:abl_agent_size}). Transformer-based agents benefit more from scaling the LLM backbone ($4\text{B}{\to}14\text{B}$) than SE-ResNet agents, with the gap largest on representation-intensive tasks (Interest: $3.1\times$; Commentary: $2.7\times$). Architecture, scale, and projector compatibility are confounded; disentangling them is future work (\cref{sec:app_abl_scaling_both_axes}).

\paragraph{Projection token count.}
We vary the number of state tokens per invocation, $k \in \{4, 8, 16, 32, 64\}$. Performance saturates at $k{=}32$; $k{=}16$ already reaches 97\% of the $k{=}32$ average (\cref{tab:abl_tokens}). Doubling to $k{=}64$ adds no gain but increases context length by 28\%. We use $k{=}32$ throughout.

\paragraph{Gameplay.}
Skilled players ($n{=}12$, all ${\geq}1300$ Elo) cannot reliably distinguish LLAMIA from a human opponent: detection falls below chance (\cref{fig:human_gameplay_main}), and post-game ratings place LLAMIA alongside Maia* on perceived human-likeness (\cref{fig:human_survey_main}). Maia is trained directly on millions of human-Elo move distributions; LLAMIA receives no human-move supervision. Instead, behavioral signatures such as time-pressure blunders and skill-appropriate piece saliency emerge from latent-state conditioning alone. LLAMIA-Verb, trained with the same backbone and DAPO budget, is detected at rates well above chance, consistent with the stylistic regularities that verbal summaries impose on move selection.

\paragraph{Commentary.}
Both systems achieve comparable factual accuracy: material balance, initiative assessment, and basic evaluations survive verbalization reasonably well. The gap concentrates on strategic insight (\cref{fig:human_commentary_main}), where participants rate LLAMIA higher by a wider margin on the Insight dimension than on Accuracy. Text preserves \emph{what} is happening on the board, but explaining \emph{why} a move is strong requires representational features (policy gradients, value topology, look-ahead depth) that do not survive verbal compression.

\paragraph{Latent tokens are instruction-modulated.}
On a fixed back-rank mate position (\cref{fig:human_heatmap}), LLAMIA's attention over the latent tokens shifts with the target Elo: at 2000 it concentrates on the mating geometry, at 1100 it disperses to material. The latent representation is identical in both cases; what changes is the LLM's reading, conditioned on the natural-language Elo instruction. The projected state functions as a perceptual input shaped by task context, not a static feature vector.
