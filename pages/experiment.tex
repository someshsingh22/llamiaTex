\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate LLAMIA on \textsc{LLAMIA-Bench}, a suite of seven tasks spanning four collaboration facets: behavioral imitation, state assessment, explanation, and instruction-conditioned play (\cref{sec:app_bench}). Across all tasks, we compare three integration regimes under matched conditions: latent internalization (LLAMIA), RL-trained verbalized tool use (LLAMIA-Verb), and a frontier model with verbalized Lc0 access (GPT-5.1\,+\,Lc0). Full dataset descriptions, metrics, and per-task prompts appear in Appendices~\ref{sec:app_bench}--\ref{sec:app_prompts}.

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:exp_setup}

\paragraph{Models.}
LLAMIA uses Qwen3-14B as the backbone with Lc0-BT4 as the subagent. We train at three scales (4B, 8B, 14B) to study size effects. Stage~1 aligns the projector on 5M state--policy pairs (\cref{sec:app_stage1}); Stage~2 runs DAPO on multi-task rollouts across all seven tasks jointly. Training details and hyperparameters are in Appendix~\ref{sec:app_implementation}.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
We compare against five groups:
(i)~\textbf{Qwen3-14B\,+\,Lc0}: same backbone as LLAMIA with Lc0-BT4 via ReAct~\cite{yao2022react} but no task-specific training, isolating the contribution of DAPO;
(ii)~\textbf{GPT-5.1\,+\,Lc0}: strongest frontier model with verbalized Lc0 access (\cref{sec:app_baseline_frontier});
(iii)~\textbf{LLAMIA-Verb}: same backbone, DAPO recipe, and rollout budget, but verbalized text instead of projected state tokens---the controlled ablation isolating the integration interface ;
(iv)~\textbf{Dedicated task finetunes}: Allie, CF-270M, SCC, trained on $10$--$100\times$ more task-specific data (\cref{sec:app_baseline_experts});
(v)~\textbf{ChessGPT}~\cite{feng2024chessgpt}: language-only fine-tuning on chess notation (7B and 13B variants).

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Each task has a primary metric detailed in \cref{sec:app_bench}: move-matching accuracy (behavior cloning), Spearman $\rho$ (difficulty and interest), G-eval and BLEU-2 (annotation and commentary), and tournament Elo (instruction-conditioned play). All reported values include 95\% bootstrap confidence intervals.

Panels~(a) and~(b) of Figure~\ref{fig:combined} show training dynamics.
The aggregate reward gap reaches $2$--$3\times$ by convergence and widens throughout training; scaling from 4B to 14B does not reduce the gap for LLAMIA-Verb.
The per-task breakdown reveals where the bottleneck bites hardest: LLAMIA-Verb achieves partial reward on behavior cloning (RL learns skill-conditional mappings from text) and difficulty (solution depth is verbalizable), but stays near-flat on puzzle interest and game commentary.
These two tasks share a single cause: the reward signal encodes structure absent from any text representation of the engine's output.

Panel~(c) summarizes the evaluation.
\textbf{(1)}~The backbone alone is insufficient: Qwen3-14B\,+\,Lc0 (no DAPO) averages~16, below LLAMIA-Verb~(31) and GPT-5.1\,+\,Lc0~(36).
\textbf{(2)}~Internalization outperforms verbalization on every task; the gap is smallest on single-position tasks and largest on interest, commentary, and ICP---consistent with panel~(b).
\textbf{(3)}~RL on verbalized tools has a ceiling: LLAMIA-Verb improves over GPT-5.1\,+\,Lc0 on annotation but \emph{underperforms} it on commentary and ICP, where query-response fragmentation hurts multi-step narratives.
\textbf{(4)}~Task finetunes are brittle OOD: Allie leads on in-distribution Maia buckets but collapses on GM-25, Low-Time, and $\Delta$Elo, where LLAMIA leads by $6.3$\,pp (\cref{tab:bc_extended}).

\subsection{The Verbalization Debt}
\label{sec:results_vd}

We define the \textbf{Verbalization Debt} $\mathrm{VD}(\mathcal{T}) = \sigma_{\mathrm{latent}}(\mathcal{T}) - \sigma_{\mathrm{verb}}(\mathcal{T})$ as the per-task score difference between internalized and verbalized integration under matched training.

\paragraph{Non-verbalizability.}
Some agent signals have no faithful text representation regardless of interaction depth. The cleanest evidence comes from puzzle understanding, where both difficulty and interest are predicted from the \emph{same} positions using the \emph{same} engine and backbone. The VD on interest ($\Delta\rho = 0.47$) is $5.2\times$ larger than on difficulty ($\Delta\rho = 0.09$). Difficulty correlates with verbalizable features (solution depth, evaluation swing); interest reflects aesthetic and psychological engagement encoded in the engine's activations but absent from any text serialization. The same divergence is visible in Figure~\ref{fig:combined}(b): LLAMIA-Verb's reward curve for Interest is flat throughout training while its curve for Difficulty shows partial improvement.

\paragraph{Alternative explanation: optimization ease.}
One might attribute the VD to optimization dynamics rather than information content---perhaps continuous state tokens are simply easier for DAPO to optimize than discrete text. The verbalization-density ablation (\cref{tab:abl_verb_spectrum}) rules this out for non-verbalizable tasks: increasing the text density from sparse (top-1 move) to dense (full policy distribution serialized as text) does not improve Interest prediction ($\rho \in [0.06, 0.08]$) despite providing strictly more information to the optimizer. The missing signal is absent from text at any density, not merely harder to extract.

    \caption{\textbf{Verbalization debt constrains collaboration diversity.}
    Both systems overlap until ${\sim}40\%$; LLAMIA rises to $H = 1.53$ (95\% of maximum) while LLAMIA-Verb plateaus at $0.90$ (56\%).}
    \label{fig:agency_diversity}
\end{figure}
The verbalization debt measured in \S\ref{sec:results_vd} is a score gap. A second consequence is behavioral: it constrains the \emph{collaboration repertoire} the model can learn. We classify 500 evaluation episodes per system into five collaboration patterns via GPT-4o ($\kappa = 0.78$; taxonomy in \cref{sec:abl_agency_strategies}): engine-follow, consult-then-override, counterfactual query, multi-step lookahead, and abstention.

At convergence, LLAMIA distributes across all five (Figure~\ref{fig:agency_diversity}); LLAMIA-Verb concentrates on engine-follow (69\%). The divergence begins after ${\sim}40\%$ of training, when LLAMIA develops two patterns absent from LLAMIA-Verb: \emph{counterfactual query} (24\%), where the model invokes the agent on hypothetical positions and compares latent representations; and \emph{multi-step lookahead} (13\%), which chains such queries to evaluate candidate plans. Together these account for 37\% of LLAMIA episodes and $<$3\% of LLAMIA-Verb episodes. Strategies also specialize by task ($\overline{\mathrm{JSD}}$: $0.12$ vs.\ $0.012$ for LLAMIA-Verb), which uses the same engine-follow profile regardless of task. This repertoire requires Stage~2 joint optimization: Stage~1 alone (projector alignment, LLM frozen) produces only engine-follow behavior, because the LLM cannot learn \emph{when} to invoke or how to compose responses across invocations without RL (\cref{sec:app_ablations}).

\label{sec:results_ablations}
\label{sec:results_skill_discovery}
\paragraph{Skill discovery via ICP.}
Instruction-conditioned planning (ICP) tests whether a model can steer play toward natural-language directives about opponent weaknesses. ICP is a test-only task, so improvement reflects transfer from training.
LLAMIA-14B achieves $2262\,(\pm 28)$ average tournament Elo across five weakness categories, versus $2150\,(\pm 32)$ for GPT-5.1\,+\,Lc0 and $2091\,(\pm 34)$ for LLAMIA-Verb (\cref{tab:icp_extended}).
Motif categories emerge in a structured order during training: pawn structures first, then piece coordination, tactical patterns, endgames, each with a sigmoid-like jump (\cref{sec:app_results_icp_motif}). The ordering mirrors chess pedagogy, consistent with latent projection enabling curriculum-like acquisition. ICP depends on the counterfactual-query and multi-step-lookahead patterns that only LLAMIA develops. Additional ablations (LoRA rank sensitivity, dual-axis scaling, failure-mode taxonomy) appear in \cref{sec:app_ablations}.

We validate the automated metrics with two human studies on $n=12$ skilled chess players (all rated ${\geq}1300$; study design in \cref{sec:app_human}).

\paragraph{Behavior cloning: what the model sees.}
Figure~\ref{fig:human_heatmap} visualizes the attention difference between LLAMIA conditioned at 2000 vs.\ 1100~Elo on a back-rank mate position (Lichess puzzle dxLpe). At 2000~Elo, attention concentrates on the mating geometry---the target square (e1), the trapped king (g1), and the confining pawns (f2/g2/h2). At 1100~Elo, attention disperses to material and central squares, missing the back-rank threat entirely. This attention shift confirms that the projected latent state carries skill-level-specific positional understanding, not merely the engine's top move---the same signal that drives behavior-cloning accuracy across Elo buckets (\cref{tab:bc_extended}).

\paragraph{Gameplay identification.}
Participants correctly identified LLAMIA-14B as AI in 39\% of trials, below the 50\% chance level (Figure~\ref{fig:human_gameplay_main}). LLAMIA-Verb-14B was detected at 72\%, well above chance despite identical backbone and training budget. Post-game surveys corroborate this: LLAMIA matches Maia*'s human-likeness ratings without training on human-move distributions (Figure~\ref{fig:human_survey_main}). The detection gap isolates the integration interface: verbalization imposes stylistic regularities (consistent avoidance of dubious moves, uniform tempo) that participants identify as non-human.

\paragraph{Commentary quality.}
LLAMIA-14B was preferred in 72.2\% of 180 commentary judgments (Figure~\ref{fig:human_commentary_main}). Mean Insight scores (4.2 vs.\ 2.5) show a larger gap than Accuracy (4.3 vs.\ 3.2), consistent with verbalization preserving surface facts but compressing the strategic rationale encoded in the agent's activations. State-annotation accuracy ($r$ with Stockfish evaluation) widens monotonically from $\Delta r = 0.03$ on simple positions to $\Delta r = 0.41$ on complex positions (\cref{sec:app_human_results}).
