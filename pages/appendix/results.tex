\paragraph{Why Allie wins on buckets but loses on wild splits.}
Allie-Adaptive-Search achieves state-of-the-art move-matching accuracy on the Maia benchmark buckets (1100--1900) by training on 93M blitz games with continuous Elo conditioning and time-adaptive MCTS~\cite{zhang2024humanalignedchessbitsearch}.
The gains over Maia are real and consistent across the entire skill spectrum.
However, the same paper reports a characteristic cluster of failure modes in online play that map precisely to our wild splits.
First, \emph{late-game blunders}: players at 1998--2038 Elo independently noted sudden piece drops with no tactical justification, and remarked that Allie ``doesn't know what to do in the endgame.''
Second, \emph{no strategic plan in the middlegame}: players at 1940 Elo observed ``non-obvious moves with no clear plan.''
Third, \emph{incorrect piece-trade decisions when losing}: players at 2004 Elo noted Allie's ``willingness to trade when down a full piece''---a failure to complicate and create counter-chances that strong human players rely on.
Fourth, \emph{blitz-data bias}: training on one year of blitz games makes Allie poorly calibrated for slow-game reasoning and GM-style positional play~\cite{zhang2024humanalignedchessbitsearch}.

These four failure modes explain the wild-split pattern in Table~\ref{tab:bc_extended}.
GM-25 exposes the planning and endgame gaps: top grandmasters play strategically complex slow-game maneuvers that Allie's blitz-trained policy cannot imitate.
Low-Time exposes the forcing-line failure: under clock pressure, humans commit instantly to forcing sequences; Allie's think-time predictor sometimes assigns long ponder times to positions with only one reasonable move~\cite{zhang2024humanalignedchessbitsearch}.
$\Delta$Elo exposes the piece-trade and complication failure: large rating-gap positions frequently involve defensive resourcefulness (stalemate traps, perpetual-check sacrifices) that requires reading the \emph{shape} of the opponent's policy distribution, not just their nominal Elo.

LLAMIA's advantage on wild splits is not a data artifact.
LLAMIA reads Lc0's latent state directly---including policy entropy over endgame continuations, piece-safety gradients, and anti-simplification features---rather than imitating move sequences.
This is why LLAMIA generalizes to endgame and complication positions where Allie's surface-level imitation breaks down.
