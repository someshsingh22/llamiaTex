\section{Extended Results}
\label{sec:app_results}
The five tasks in LLAMIA-Bench probe different regimes of LLM–agent collaboration, varying in horizon, evaluation metrics, and the strength of task-specific baselines. Here we discuss the extended per-task evaluation of LLAMIA with more metrics, baselines, and detailed analysis of LLAMIA's task specific behavior.


\subsection{Behavior Cloning}
\label{sec:app_results_bc}
Behavior Cloning asks the system to predict the move a human at a given skill level would play rather than the optimal move, given the position and the target Elo rating.

We report two splits. The first is the \textbf{MAIA Test split}: five Elo buckets ($1100$, $1300$, $1500$, $1700$, $1900$) drawn from Lichess blitz, matching the protocol of \citet{mcilroy2022aligning} and the in-distribution setting for the published BC experts.
The second is \textbf{Wild}, three out-of-distribution splits we introduce.
\circled{\textbf{1}} \textit{GM-25}: contains top-grandmaster games \circled{\textbf{2}}
\textit{Low-Time}: contains positions played with under 30 seconds remaining, players often play different move when they or opponents are under time pressure. \circled{\textbf{3}}
\textit{$\Delta$Elo}: contains games with large rating gaps between the two players leading to different attacking or defensive strategies.

\subsection{Move Annotation \& Game Commentary}
\label{sec:app_results_rationale}
\label{sec:app_results_gamecomm}
Move Annotation and Game Commentary are language generation tasks that require the system to generate explanations or commentate on moves played by a player or game segment between two players. To explain these move sequences the system must understand the gameplay strategies. Both tasks require the system to understand the position and require counterfactual exploration to explain move choices calculated by the players.

\subsubsection{Move Annotation} requires the agent to deduce the intent or rationale behind a player's move made in a given position-- what is this move trying to achieve?-- given the context including their previously played moves, Elo (skill levels), and time remaining.

We train all baselines and LLAMIA on both tasks and evaluate by G-eval on relevance, completeness, clarity, and fluency (the same G-eval also acts as the DAPO reward, \cref{sec:app_bench_commentary}) We also report BLEU-2 scores alongside G-eval for game level commentary, to make an LLM as a judge free metric.

\paragraph{Importance calibration in qualitative outputs.}
Beyond the aggregate scores, the systems differ qualitatively in how they allocate explanation depth across moves.
LLM-Only (Qwen3-14B+Lc0) and GPT-5.1 produce near-uniform-length commentary: every move receives 2--3 sentences regardless of whether it is a routine development move or a critical sacrifice.
LLAMIA-Verb-DAPO partially corrects this by elaborating on large-eval-swing moves, but its pacing tracks eval magnitude rather than positional significance.
It over-emphasises $0.1$--$0.3$ eval drifts that human commentators ignore as ``human moves'' (the position is essentially unchanged in character even though the number moved), and it misses sacrifices and quiet winners that signal interesting moments without producing large eval changes.
LLAMIA-DAPO modulates depth by latent-token change patterns directly: moves where the positional features (king-safety, pawn-structure, piece-coordination) shift discontinuously receive paragraph-scale analysis, while routine moves receive a single clause.

\subsection{Puzzle Understanding}
\label{sec:app_results_puzzle}
Puzzle Understanding evaluates whether a system can use the agent's state to judge a tactical position rather than only select the engine move.
We report two single-position ranking tasks.
\emph{Difficulty} asks the system to order puzzles by empirical Lichess solve difficulty.
\emph{Interest} asks it to order positions by the fraction of users who mark them interesting.
Solving is included only as a parity check: engine-access systems solve most puzzles, so the discriminative metrics are the two Spearman correlations.

\subsubsection{Difficulty and Interest prediction}
\label{sec:app_results_puzzle_prediction}
Difficulty has a partial verbal proxy in solution length, which appears in the principal variation (PV).
Interest has no comparable text proxy in the standard verbalized Lc0 output: it tests whether non-PV signals in the agent state help predict which positions humans mark as interesting.
The matched LLAMIA-Verb and LLAMIA rows in \cref{tab:puzzle_understanding_extended} therefore test how much of the agent state survives verbalization at fixed position set, subagent, backbone scale, and optimization recipe.

\subsection{Gameplay (Parity Metric)}
\label{sec:app_results_gameplay}

Playing strength is reported as a parity check rather than a headline result.
Any system that can call Lc0 and pass through its top move reaches the same Elo band, so gameplay does not separate the systems we care about separating.
We include it to confirm that the LLAMIA-Bench tasks above are not measuring a system that can no longer play chess.

\subsubsection{Action Ranking (Kendall's $\tau$)}
\label{sec:app_results_gameplay_kt}

LLAMIA's policy alignment with the search-augmented agent (Lc0-BT4 with $800$-node search) is measured via Kendall's $\tau$ over the top-$10$ move ranking.
Alignment increases during training and stabilises around $30$--$50$M training positions;
the full curve appears in the training-dynamics figure.

\subsubsection{Playing Strength (Elo)}
\label{sec:app_results_gameplay_elo}

All search-free systems with engine access cluster at $2200$--$2300$ Elo, confirming that gameplay is a parity metric once the agent's top-move recommendation is available.
LLAMIA-$14$B achieves $2279 \pm 45$ Elo, matching the 270M Transformer baseline of \citet{ruoss2024grandmaster}.
The gap to a system that does not have engine access (Qwen3-$14$B alone is well below 1500 Elo) is large;
the gap among systems that do is within noise.

\subsection{Synthesis: How the Results Test the Landscape}
\label{sec:app_results_synthesis}

The per-task tables above test concrete predictions, and the cross-task pattern matches the mechanisms predicted in \cref{sec:app_landscape_cross}, with one metric-dependent refinement in the placement of Difficulty.

\paragraph{Verb--latent gap, ordered by relative gain.}
Because the per-task metrics differ (Spearman, accuracy, BLEU-2, G-eval), we use the latent-DAPO-$14$B to verbal-DAPO-$14$B ratio as a within-task comparator and cross-check it with the diagonal comparison LLAMIA-$4$B vs.\ LLAMIA-Verb-$14$B.
Interest is the largest gap by a wide margin ($\rho=0.08\to0.52$, $6.5\times$), consistent with the claim that the discriminative signal lives in the policy distribution and value gradients that verbalization discards.
Commentary follows ($0.40\to0.75$ G-eval, $1.9\times$), consistent with per-position perceptual debt compounding across the game.
Difficulty ($0.45\to0.71$, $1.6\times$) sits below Commentary but above BC and Rationale in the realized metrics: solution length gives the verbal channel a real proxy, yet the latent residual over motifs, forced-move structure, and distractor sharpness is large enough that LLAMIA-$4$B still beats LLAMIA-Verb-$14$B ($0.58$ vs.\ $0.45$).
Rationale ($33.2\to45.8$ BLEU-2, $1.4\times$) and Behavior Cloning ($45\to53$ MAIA, $1.18\times$; $39\to49$ Wild, $1.26\times$) have smaller gaps because the verbal channel already covers much of the target: PV-derived inference covers most of Planning, and top-$k$ engine moves cover most human BC moves.
The diagonal check gives the same ordering of interface pressure: latent-$4$B beats verbal-$14$B by $+30$ Spearman on Interest, $+15$ G-eval on Commentary, $+13$ Spearman on Difficulty, $+4$ BLEU-2 on Rationale, and $+5/+4$ pp on BC MAIA/Wild.

\paragraph{SFT--DAPO gap, ordered by task horizon.}
Commentary shows the largest SFT$\to$DAPO gain ($0.58\to0.75$ G-eval at latent-$14$B, plus the largest verbal SFT$\to$DAPO gain in the benchmark), because RL teaches narrative pacing, counterfactual exploration, and critical-moment detection from latent-token changes.
Annotation is next on the Comparative subcategory ($+10.3$\,pp under latent vs.\ $+2.6$ under verbal at $14$B), where RL discovers the counterfactual-query strategy.
BC and Difficulty are far smaller: BC gains $+1$--$3$\,pp at $14$B depending on split and interface, while Difficulty gains $+0.05$ Spearman under both interfaces.
Interest remains the smallest optimization effect ($0.48\to0.52$ under latent and $0.07\to0.08$ under verbalization), because single-step prediction does not activate RL's sequencing advantage.
The interaction between interface and optimization is super-additive on Annotation and Commentary and additive on BC, Difficulty, and Interest, exactly the partition the landscape analysis predicts.

\paragraph{Integration interface dominates backbone scale.}
At fixed backbone, the verb$\to$latent switch is consistently larger than the $4$B$\to$$14$B scaling step at fixed interface.
On Interest, latent gives $+0.44$ Spearman at $14$B while $4$B$\to$$14$B scaling gives $+0.14$ under latent and only $+0.03$ under verbalization.
On Difficulty, latent gives $+0.26$ Spearman at $14$B, compared with $+0.13$ latent scaling and $+0.07$ verbal scaling.
The same diagonal pattern is smaller but still present on BC and Rationale: LLAMIA-$4$B beats LLAMIA-Verb-$14$B by $+5/+4$ pp on BC MAIA/Wild and by $+4$ BLEU-2 on Rationale.
Commentary is the larger multi-step case: the interface switch gives $+0.35$ G-eval at $14$B, while latent scaling gives $+0.20$.
The pattern extends the BC-literature finding (Chessformer-79M beats GPT-3.5-175B on move-matching at $2000\times$ fewer parameters~\cite{XXX_chessformer}) to a setting where the backbone architecture is held constant and only the information channel between the LLM and the subagent changes.

\paragraph{Strong baselines fail in their predicted modes.}
Allie-Adaptive-Search loses on every Wild split despite holding the highest single in-distribution Maia bucket, with the failure modes traced back to blitz-data bias in its training distribution.
GPT-5.1\,+\,Lc0 is the strongest non-LLAMIA system on Difficulty, Annotation, and Commentary, but gets no help from the verbalized tool on Interest, where the signal is non-verbalizable.
GPT-5.1 text-only sits below older non-chat LLMs on BC because of chat-tuning artefacts.
SCC is competitive on Comparative annotation (pattern matching) and weak on Planning (no engine lookahead in its training signal).
Each of these failures is specific and predicted by the landscape analysis;
none requires invoking a generic ``chess is hard'' explanation.

The remaining question, whether this picture transfers to a non-chess pretrained agent with extractable representations, is the one the conclusion takes up.

\paragraph{Reading the headline numbers.}
Three structural patterns connect the columns; the per-task tables in the subsections below verify each one.

\emph{Verbalization is lossy, and the loss is task-specific.}
At fixed backbone (Qwen3-14B), fixed subagent (Lc0-BT4), and fixed training recipe (DAPO), replacing the verbalized text channel with latent tokens lifts every column.
The relative gain follows the landscape mechanism in \cref{sec:app_landscape_cross}: largest on Interest ($\rho{=}0.08{\to}0.52$, a $6.5\times$ jump), where the discriminative signal lives in the policy distribution and the value gradients across candidate moves, none of which the verbalized output carries;
large on Commentary ($0.40{\to}0.75$), where positional mechanisms compound across the game;
moderate on Difficulty ($\rho{=}0.45{\to}0.71$), where solution length is a partial verbal proxy but leaves a residual signal over motif type and distractor sharpness;
smaller on Rationale ($33{\to}46$), where PV-derived inference covers much of Planning;
and smallest on Behavior Cloning ($45{\to}53$ on MAIA, $39{\to}49$ on Wild), where the engine's top-$k$ moves cover most of what humans actually play.
The matched-recipe LLAMIA-Verb-$\{$4B,8B,14B$\}$ rows isolate this ordering from confounds: the only variable that changes across the LLAMIA-Verb${\to}$LLAMIA boundary is the integration interface.

\emph{Internalization is perceptual; agency is multi-step.}
The gain from latent tokens splits into two components that surface in different task families.
On single-step prediction (Interest, Difficulty, BC), the latent advantage is mostly perceptual: latent SFT alone closes most of the verb--latent gap, and DAPO adds little on top.
The per-task tables make this concrete: LLAMIA-SFT-14B already reaches $\rho{=}0.48$ on Interest (vs.\ verbal DAPO at $0.08$), and adding RL lifts it only to $0.52$.
On multi-step tasks (Annotation, Commentary), DAPO becomes load-bearing because RL discovers collaboration strategies that are structurally unproductive under verbalization: counterfactual queries (play the alternative move, re-invoke the subagent on the resulting position, compare the two latent states), feature reading (attend to specific king-safety or pawn-structure activations to ground an explanation), and narrative pacing across 30+ moves.
The interface and the optimization procedure are super-additive on these tasks; the per-task $2{\times}2$ ablations isolate the interaction term, and \cref{sec:app_landscape_cross} ties the ordering to task horizon.

\emph{Strong baselines do not close the gap.}
Two competitive non-LLAMIA systems appear in the table, each strong on a different subset of tasks.
Allie-Adaptive-Search~\cite{zhang2024humanalignedchessbitsearch} is the dedicated BC expert, trained on $93$M Lichess-blitz games with continuous skill conditioning and time-adaptive MCTS at inference.
It wins the highest in-distribution Maia bucket ($58$\% at $1900$, \cref{tab:bc_extended}) and leads the unweighted MAIA average ($55$ vs.\ LLAMIA-14B's $53$), but trails LLAMIA-14B on every Wild split, where its blitz-only training distribution covers neither slow-game GM play nor severe time pressure nor large rating-gap dynamics.
GPT-5.1\,+\,Lc0 is the strongest non-LLAMIA system on Difficulty, Annotation, and Commentary, recovering most of the verbal-channel ceiling through aggressive tool use ($10{+}$ calls per move on Commentary) and long-context reasoning over evaluation trajectories.
It still trails LLAMIA-14B by $8$ to $23$ points on BC, Difficulty, Rationale, and Commentary.
Interest is the sharper failure mode: adding the verbalized tool does not improve over the same model without it ($\rho{=}0.10$ with Lc0 vs.\ $0.12$ text-only), while LLAMIA-14B reaches $\rho{=}0.52$.
On tasks where the discriminative signal is non-verbalizable, reasoning over a lossy interface cannot recover what the interface discarded.
