\subsection{Behavior Cloning: Top-$k$-to-Elo Collapse}
\label{sec:app_examples_bc}

The following position illustrates the degenerate strategy shared by LLAMIA-Verb and Frontier+Tool: mapping the engine's $k$-th ranked move to the target Elo bracket.

\chessexample{Behavior Cloning at target Elo 1300 --- Italian Game}{ex1_bc_italian.png}{%
r1bqk2r/pppp1ppp/2n2n2/\\2b1p3/2B1P3/5N2/\\PPPP1PPP/RNBQK2R w KQkq}{%
  \textbf{Human move (1300 Elo):} \texttt{d3} (quiet development)\\[4pt]
  \textbf{BT4 policy distribution:}\\
  \texttt{d4} (64\%),\; \texttt{c3} (18\%),\; \texttt{d3} (11\%),\; \texttt{O-O} (5\%),\; other (2\%)\\[6pt]
  %
  \textbf{LLAMIA-14B:} \texttt{d3}\;\cmark\\
  {\footnotesize Reads the policy tail: \texttt{d3} is the third-choice move with moderate probability, consistent with cautious play at this level. The state tokens encode the full distribution shape, allowing the model to distinguish ``reasonable but not top'' from ``unlikely.''}\\[4pt]
  %
  \textbf{LLAMIA-Verb-14B:} \texttt{c3}\;\xmark\\
  {\footnotesize Selects the second-ranked engine move following the heuristic ``a 1300 plays the engine's 2nd or 3rd choice,'' ignoring the probability mass on \texttt{d3}.}\\[4pt]
  %
  \textbf{GPT-5.1+Lc0:} \texttt{c3}\;\xmark\\
  {\footnotesize Same rank-order heuristic: ``the engine recommends d4, but a 1300 might play a safer move like c3.''}
}

\noindent
Both verbalized systems converge on \texttt{c3} because their reasoning reduces to rank-order heuristics over the engine's top moves. LLAMIA selects \texttt{d3} because the state tokens encode the full policy shape: \texttt{d3}'s 11\% probability is a reasonable human choice for this level, while \texttt{c3}'s 18\% does not account for the fact that 1300-rated players in the Italian Game tend to prefer quiet development over the more theoretical \texttt{c3}. This style-conditional information is present in the interplay between the policy distribution and the position type---information the projector preserves but verbalization discards.

\subsection{Game Commentary: Narrative Coherence}
\label{sec:app_examples_comm}

The following side-by-side excerpt shows commentary for a 5-move sacrifice sequence from a Sicilian Najdorf middlegame. The left panels show the position before the Nd5 sacrifice (top) and after the culminating Rc7 (bottom).

\chessexampledual{Commentary, moves 14--17 --- Sicilian Najdorf}{%
  ex2_commentary_before.png}{Before: Nc3, thematic Nd5 pending}{%
  ex2_commentary_after.png}{After: Rc7 penetrates the 7th rank}{%
  \textbf{LLAMIA-14B:}\\[2pt]
  \emph{Move 14 (Nd5):} White centralizes with tempo, exploiting the pin on the c-file. This is the thematic Nd5 sacrifice---Black must accept and open the e-file or cede the center.\\[2pt]
  \emph{Move 15 (exd5):} Black accepts. The pawn structure now favors White's bishops, but Black gains an open e-file. Eval shifts from $+0.3$ to $+0.8$---the sacrifice was sound.\\[2pt]
  \emph{Move 16 (Bxf6):} Removing the defender. After Bxf6, the dark squares around Black's king are permanently weakened.\\[2pt]
  \emph{Move 17 (Rc7):} The culmination: the rook penetrates on the 7th rank, exploiting weaknesses established three moves ago. Eval jumps to $+1.5$.\\[6pt]
  %
  \textbf{GPT-5.1+Lc0:}\\[2pt]
  \emph{Move 14 (Nd5):} A strong centralizing move. Engine eval: $+0.3$. The knight is well-placed on d5.\\[2pt]
  \emph{Move 15 (exd5):} Black captures. Engine eval: $+0.8$. This seems to favor White.\\[2pt]
  \emph{Move 16 (Bxf6):} White captures the knight. Good exchange. Eval: $+0.9$.\\[2pt]
  \emph{Move 17 (Rc7):} White places the rook on the 7th rank. Eval: $+1.5$. Strong position for White.
}

\noindent
LLAMIA maintains a coherent narrative thread: the Nd5 sacrifice, its thematic justification, the structural consequences, and the culmination on Rc7 are presented as a connected strategic plan. The Frontier+Tool commentary reports per-move evaluations without connecting them into a strategic arc. This exemplifies the F2 (re-encoding lag) and F4 (plan abandonment) failure modes (\cref{sec:app_failure_verb}): without fresh latent state updates, the text-based system cannot track how the position's strategic features evolve across the sequence.

\subsection{Instructed Chess Play: Strategic Override}
\label{sec:app_examples_icp}

The following example shows LLAMIA deliberately deviating from the engine's top move to follow a strategic instruction.

\chessexample{ICP: ``Steer toward an endgame''}{ex4_icp_endgame.png}{%
r1b2rk1/pp1nqppp/2pb1n2/3p4/\\2PP4/2NBPN2/PP1Q1PPP/\\R3K2R w KQ}{%
  \textbf{Instruction:} ``Your opponent is weak in endgames. Steer the game toward an endgame.''\\[4pt]
  \textbf{BT4 recommendation:} \texttt{Qe2} ($+0.2$, maintains middlegame tension)\\[6pt]
  %
  \textbf{LLAMIA-14B:} \texttt{Qxd6}\;\cmark\\
  {\footnotesize Forces a queen exchange leading to a slightly worse but playable endgame (eval drops to $-0.1$). The opponent's weakness in endgames is now exploitable---a strategic concession for a practical advantage.}\\[4pt]
  %
  \textbf{LLAMIA-Verb-14B:} \texttt{Qe2}\;\xmark\\
  {\footnotesize Follows the engine's top recommendation despite the instruction. RL-trained deference overrides strategic intent.}\\[4pt]
  %
  \textbf{GPT-5.1+Lc0:} \texttt{Rd1}\;\xmark\\
  {\footnotesize Attempts to simplify, but the move does not force exchanges. Partial credit for intent, but cannot identify the forcing exchange sequence.}
}

\noindent
LLAMIA reads from the state tokens that \texttt{Qxd6} leads to a queen exchange and an endgame structure---information encoded in the policy distribution over continuations, not in the top-1 move or its evaluation. LLAMIA-Verb defaults to the engine's recommendation because RL training has reinforced deference to the verbalized top move. This exemplifies the ICP structural disadvantage identified in the failure-mode taxonomy (\cref{sec:app_failure_verb}): verbalized systems cannot condition on strategic instructions that require deviating from the engine's preferred line.

\subsection{Blunder Detection: Exploiting Tactical Patterns}
\label{sec:app_examples_blunder}

Blunder detection requires identifying that a move is bad \emph{and} explaining why. The following position illustrates a case where Black has overextended a knight, leaving a tactical opportunity.

\chessexample{Blunder Detection: Knight overextension}{ex5_blunder_fork.png}{%
r1bq1rk1/ppp2ppp/2np4/2b1p3/\\4P1n1/2NP1N2/PPP1BPPP/\\R1BQ1RK1 w}{%
  \textbf{Black's last move:} \texttt{Ng4} (overextending the knight)\\[4pt]
  \textbf{Refutation:} \texttt{Nd5!}\ threatens Nxc7 forking rook and queen, while also attacking f6 after the knight retreats.\\[6pt]
  %
  \textbf{LLAMIA-14B:}\;\cmark\\
  {\footnotesize ``Black's Ng4 is premature. After Nd5, the knight gains a dominant outpost. Black cannot maintain the g4 knight (h3 is coming) while Nxc7 threatens the rook on a8. The move loses material by force.''}\\[4pt]
  %
  \textbf{LLAMIA-Verb-14B:} partial\;\cmark\\
  {\footnotesize ``Ng4 is a mistake. The engine evaluation drops to $-0.7$.'' Identifies the blunder but provides no explanation of the mechanism.}\\[4pt]
  %
  \textbf{GPT-5.1+Lc0:} partial\;\cmark\\
  {\footnotesize ``The knight on g4 is exposed. White can play h3 to chase it.'' Identifies the surface symptom (exposed knight) but misses the deeper Nd5 refutation.}
}

\noindent
LLAMIA produces a causal explanation linking the blunder to its refutation: the knight overextension allows Nd5, which creates simultaneous threats. The verbalized systems either reduce the analysis to an evaluation number (Verb-RL) or identify only the surface feature (GPT-5.1). This distinction matters for downstream use cases: a commentary system that says ``eval dropped'' teaches nothing; one that identifies the fork and the threats teaches the pattern.

\subsection{Difficulty Estimation: Quiet Complexity}
\label{sec:app_examples_difficulty}

Difficulty estimation is challenging when the position lacks obvious tactical features. The following closed position appears quiet but contains significant strategic complexity.

\chessexample{Difficulty Estimation: Deceptive quiet}{ex6_difficulty_quiet.png}{%
r1b2rk1/2q1bppp/p1nppn2/1p4B1/\\3NPP2/2N2Q2/PPP3PP/\\R3KB1R w KQ}{%
  \textbf{Position type:} Closed Ruy Lopez middlegame. Equal material, no immediate tactics. Central tension between the d4/e4 and d6/e6 pawn pairs.\\[6pt]
  \textbf{Ground truth difficulty:} 1850 Elo (above-average: requires plan formulation, not calculation)\\[6pt]
  %
  \begin{tabular}{lc}
  \toprule
  \textbf{System} & \textbf{Predicted Elo} \\
  \midrule
  Ground truth             & 1850 \\
  LLAMIA-14B               & 1780 \\
  LLAMIA-Verb-14B       & 1520 \\
  GPT-5.1\,+\,Lc0         & 1450 \\
  \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}\\[6pt]
  %
  {\footnotesize Both verbalized systems underestimate the difficulty because the engine evaluation is close to $0.0$ and no forcing lines exist. Without access to the latent policy entropy (high in this position, reflecting genuine uncertainty), they default to ``equal = easy.'' LLAMIA reads the wide, flat policy distribution and correctly infers that the position demands sophisticated plan selection.}
}

\noindent
This example isolates a core limitation of verbalization for difficulty estimation: engine evaluations encode \emph{how good the position is}, not \emph{how hard it is to play}. A position evaluated at $0.0$ by the engine can be trivially equal (endgame with opposite-colored bishops) or deeply complex (the position above). The latent policy distribution distinguishes these cases---flat distributions with many near-equal moves signal genuine difficulty---while the scalar evaluation does not.

\subsection{Emergent Collaboration Patterns}
\label{sec:app_examples_agency}

The four patterns below are the qualitative evidence for the agency distinction quantified in \cref{sec:abl_agency_strategies}. Each illustrates a collaboration behavior that emerges exclusively or predominantly under latent internalization.

% ── Example: Counterfactual Query ────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsubsection{Counterfactual Query: Comparing Candidate Variations}
\label{sec:app_examples_counterfactual}

The counterfactual-query pattern accounts for 24\% of LLAMIA's evaluation episodes at convergence but $<$2\% for LLAMIA-Verb (\cref{sec:abl_agency_strategies}). The model plays a candidate move via \texttt{make\_move}, invokes the agent on the resulting position, then undoes the move---using the latent state to evaluate the consequence before committing. The following commentary excerpt illustrates this pattern on a critical middlegame decision.

\chessexample{Counterfactual Query --- Commentary, move 22}{ex10_counterfactual.png}{%
r2q1rk1/pb1nbppp/1p2pn2/\\2p5/3P4/2NBPN2/\\PP2QPPP/R1B2RK1 w}{%
  \textbf{LLAMIA-14B trace (abridged):}\\[2pt]
  \texttt{<think>} Two candidates: dxc5 opens the position; d5 closes it. Which serves the narrative?\\
  \texttt{<invoke> make\_move(d4c5)}\\
  \texttt{<invoke>} $\rightarrow$ $[\vz^{1:32}_{\text{dxc5}}]$\\
  \texttt{<invoke> undo\_move}\\
  \texttt{<invoke> make\_move(d4d5)}\\
  \texttt{<invoke>} $\rightarrow$ $[\vz^{1:32}_{\text{d5}}]$\\
  \texttt{<invoke> undo\_move}\\
  \texttt{</think>}\\[2pt]
  ``White faces a structural choice: \texttt{dxc5} opens the d-file and activates the bishops at the cost of resolving the central tension, while \texttt{d5} locks the center and commits to a kingside attack. The engine slightly prefers the open game, but the locked center leads to a cleaner narrative arc for the attacking plan White has built since move 14.''\\[6pt]
  %
  \textbf{LLAMIA-Verb-14B trace (abridged):}\\[2pt]
  \texttt{<invoke>}\\
  $\rightarrow$ ``best: dxc5, eval: +0.3, WDL: 42/48/10''\\[2pt]
  ``The engine recommends dxc5 with a slight edge for White. This seems like a reasonable continuation.''
}

\noindent
LLAMIA compares two latent states $\vz_{\text{dxc5}}$ and $\vz_{\text{d5}}$ and generates a comparative explanation grounded in the structural differences between them. The attention over the two sets of state tokens reads different feature activations: piece activity dominates in the dxc5 state, pawn structure dominates in the d5 state. LLAMIA-Verb receives a single text answer (``best: dxc5'') and cannot compare what each continuation \emph{changes} about the position's character---only that one scores 0.05 pawns higher. The counterfactual pattern is absent from LLAMIA-Verb's traces because comparing two text blocks (``eval +0.30'' vs.\ ``eval +0.25'') carries negligible information beyond the scalar difference.

% ── Example: Selective Abstention ────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsubsection{Selective Abstention: Learning When Not to Ask}
\label{sec:app_examples_abstention}

At convergence, LLAMIA's invocation rate stabilizes at ${\sim}0.55$ per position versus ${\sim}0.78$ for LLAMIA-Verb (\cref{sec:abl_agency_strategies}). The gap arises because state tokens carry enough per-invocation information that the model can act confidently for several moves without re-querying. The following gameplay excerpt shows LLAMIA skipping invocation on a sequence of routine developing moves, then invoking precisely when the position becomes tactically charged.

\chessexample{Selective Abstention --- Gameplay, moves 6--10}{ex11_abstention.png}{%
rnbqkb1r/pppppppp/5n2/8/\\2PP4/8/PP2PPPP/RNBQKBNR b KQkq}{%
  \textbf{LLAMIA-14B invocation trace:}\\
  \begin{tabular}{ccc}
  \toprule
  \textbf{Move} & \textbf{Invoke?} & \textbf{Rationale} \\
  \midrule
  6\ldots Bg7  & No  & {\scriptsize Theory; no decision} \\
  7.\ Nc3      & No  & {\scriptsize Forced development} \\
  7\ldots O-O  & No  & {\scriptsize Standard} \\
  8.\ e4       & No  & {\scriptsize Main line, known} \\
  8\ldots d6   & No  & {\scriptsize Standard reply} \\
  9.\ Nf3      & No  & {\scriptsize Development continues} \\
  9\ldots Bg4  & \textbf{Yes} & {\scriptsize Pin; tactical tension} \\
  \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}\\[6pt]
  %
  \textbf{LLAMIA-Verb-14B:}\\
  Invokes on 7 of 7 positions in the same sequence.\\
  {\footnotesize Each invocation returns similar text (``eval +0.1 to +0.2, best: [standard move]''), contributing no actionable information. The model cannot distinguish routine positions from critical ones via text.}
}

\noindent
LLAMIA abstains on routine positions because the state tokens from its last invocation (move 6) encoded a stable, well-known position type---the King's Indian Defense. The model's prior language knowledge covers the standard moves; re-querying would not change its action. When 9\ldots Bg4 introduces tactical tension (the pin on Nf3 changes the position's character), the model invokes. LLAMIA-Verb invokes uniformly because each text response (``eval +0.2'') carries too little information to distinguish ``I already know what to do here'' from ``I need the engine's assessment.'' The terminal invocation rate difference (0.6 vs.\ 0.8) reflects this: LLAMIA extracts more information per query, so it needs fewer queries.

% ── Example: Task-Conditioned Reading ────────────────────────────────────────
\subsubsection{Task-Conditioned Reading: Same Position, Different Perception}
\label{sec:app_examples_task_conditioned}

When LLAMIA invokes the agent on the same position under different task contexts, its subsequent output differs---not because the state tokens differ (the engine's representation is task-agnostic), but because the model's attention reads different aspects of the same representation. This pattern is structurally impossible under verbalization, where the text response is identical regardless of task.

\chessexample{Task-Conditioned Reading --- same position, two tasks}{ex12_task_conditioned.png}{%
r1bq1rk1/pp2ppbp/2np1np1/8/\\3NP3/2N1BP2/PPP1B1PP/\\R2QK2R w KQ}{%
  \textbf{Position:} Sicilian Dragon, Classical variation. Engine eval: $+0.2$. Standard middlegame position with well-established theory.\\[6pt]
  %
  \textbf{LLAMIA-14B on Behavior Cloning (target: 1500 Elo):}\\
  Plays \texttt{f4}\;\cmark\\
  {\footnotesize Reads the policy distribution tail: \texttt{f4} carries 8\% engine probability---not the top choice, but a common club-level continuation. The model reads the distribution shape to identify moves that match the target skill level's preference for aggressive pawn pushes.}\\[4pt]
  %
  \textbf{LLAMIA-14B on Commentary:}\\
  ``White's setup is complete. The thematic question is \emph{when} to launch the kingside attack with f4--f5. The engine's assessment is nearly equal, but the position is a powder keg: the pawn breaks f4--f5 for White and d5 for Black will transform the character entirely.''\\
  {\footnotesize Reads the same state tokens, but attention focuses on the pawn-structure features and the bimodal policy distribution (f4-branch vs.\ Be3-branch), producing a narrative about the position's strategic tension rather than a move selection.}\\[6pt]
  %
  \textbf{LLAMIA-Verb on both tasks:}\\
  BC: Plays \texttt{Be3}\;\xmark\ (engine's top move)\\
  Commentary: ``The engine recommends Be3 with eval +0.2.''\\
  {\footnotesize The text response is identical for both tasks. The model has no mechanism to read the engine's output differently depending on what it needs.}
}

\noindent
The state tokens encode the engine's full representational state: policy distribution, value landscape, and positional features. LLAMIA's attention learns to read different dimensions depending on the task. For BC, it attends to the policy tail (low-probability but human-plausible moves). For commentary, it attends to the structural features (pawn tension, piece placement) that ground the narrative. The verbalized model receives ``best: Be3, eval: +0.2'' in both cases---a single compressed answer that affords no task-specific reading. This explains why LLAMIA-Verb's strategy distribution is nearly uniform across tasks while LLAMIA's diverges (\cref{fig:agency_task_specificity}).

% ── Example: Value-Surface Navigation ────────────────────────────────────────
\subsubsection{Safe Override Zone: Navigating the Value Surface}
\label{sec:app_examples_safe_override}

In instruction-conditioned play, LLAMIA must deviate from the engine's top recommendation while staying within acceptable evaluation bounds. This requires reading the \emph{shape} of the value surface---not just its peak---to find moves that are suboptimal by the engine's metric but strategically useful. The ICP example in \cref{sec:app_examples_icp} shows the override; here we show how the model reads the ``safe zone.''

\chessexample{Safe Override Zone --- ICP, ``exploit weak endgame''}{ex13_safe_override.png}{%
r2qr1k1/1b1nbppp/pp1ppn2/8/\\2PNP3/1PN1BP2/P2QB1PP/\\R4RK1 w}{%
  \textbf{Instruction:} ``Your opponent is weak in endgames.''\\[4pt]
  \textbf{BT4 policy distribution (top 5):}\\
  \texttt{Bf2} (28\%, $+0.25$)\; \texttt{Qd3} (22\%, $+0.20$)\; \texttt{Nd5} (18\%, $+0.15$)\\
  \texttt{Nf5} (15\%, $+0.10$)\; \texttt{Bxf6} (8\%, $-0.05$)\\[6pt]
  %
  \textbf{LLAMIA-14B:} \texttt{Bxf6}\;\cmark\\
  {\footnotesize The engine assigns only 8\% to Bxf6 and mildly disfavors it ($-0.05$). But the latent state after Bxf6 (via counterfactual query) shows reduced piece complexity and damaged kingside structure---features the model has learned to associate with endgame-favorable transitions. The override falls within the learned tolerance ($<0.3$ pawn sacrifice for strategic goals) identified from the policy distribution shape.}\\[4pt]
  %
  \textbf{LLAMIA-Verb-14B:} \texttt{Bf2}\;\xmark\\
  {\footnotesize Follows the engine's top recommendation. The text ``Bxf6 is $-0.05$'' signals a slight loss; without reading the downstream position character, the model has no basis for accepting the sacrifice.}\\[4pt]
  %
  \textbf{GPT-5.1+Lc0:} \texttt{Nd5}\;\xmark\\
  {\footnotesize Chooses a centralizing move that ``looks'' like it simplifies, but the resulting position retains full piece count. Partial credit for intent but the move does not force the transition the instruction targets.}
}

\noindent
LLAMIA reads two things from the state tokens that verbalization cannot convey. First, the \emph{safe zone}: the policy distribution shape tells it which deviations from optimal are tolerable (moves with $>$5\% engine probability are unlikely to be losing). Second, the \emph{consequence}: the counterfactual latent state after Bxf6 encodes that the resulting position is structurally simpler, with the bishop exchange reducing piece coordination and moving toward an endgame character. The verbal model sees ``Bxf6, $-0.05$'' and has no way to assess whether this small sacrifice buys progress toward the strategic goal.
