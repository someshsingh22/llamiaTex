\subsection{Experiments}
\label{sec:todo_experiments}

\textbf{Claims.}
\textbf{C1} -- LLAMIA-Bench provides comprehensive ground-truth-anchored
evaluation of LM--specialist collaboration across six facets, in a
single environment;
\textbf{C2} (corollary) -- the verbalization debt survives matched
backbone, matched compute, and matched RL recipe -- it is an
\emph{interface} property, not a scale or recipe artefact;
\textbf{C4} -- the latent space encodes interpretable chess concepts and is
modulated by natural-language instructions, visible via linear probes and
attention analysis; LLAMIA's board-attention reproduces human expert
saliency in a skill-stratified manner across training;
\textbf{C5} -- the integration paradigm is not chess-specific;
\textbf{C6} -- LLAMIA develops emergent collaboration agency: it learns
\emph{when} to invoke the subagent and which strategies to employ,
recovering known human--engine centaur patterns and discovering novel ones.

\subsubsection*{Claim-anchor experiments [P0]}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{[C1] Full LLAMIA-Bench evaluation.} All baselines
    (Groups A--F, brief~\ref{brief:baselines}) over the six-facet
    suite; one task per facet in the main-paper table
    (\textsc{Move Classification}, \textsc{Evaluation Rationalization}, \textsc{Move Annotation},
    \textsc{Game Commentary}, \textsc{Maia Behavior Cloning}, \textsc{Instruction-Conditioned Play: Win}). Verbalization Debt / Depth-Compounding Slope scalars
    (brief~\ref{brief:metrics}) summarise the integration-interface
    corollary on this same table -- no separate experiment.
    \emph{Purpose:} the single source of truth for every quantitative
    claim downstream; the headline radar visualises this row-set.

%    Reported as one row block in the C1 table.
%    \emph{Purpose:} settles the interface-vs-scale-vs-recipe confound
%    that frontier-system comparisons (Groups A/B) cannot.

    \item \textbf{[C3] Latent vs.\ symbolic-rich, matched information
    content.} Replace $\vz_s$ with a textual representation of equal
    information content (top-$k$ moves with full eval, WDL, PVs to
    depth 20, positional features); train at matched FLOPs.
    \emph{Purpose:} the cleanest possible ablation of representation
    \emph{form} at matched information \emph{content}. If symbolic-rich
    text closes the gap, C3 fails.
    \item \textbf{[C4] Interpretability: linear probes and instruction
    modulation.}
    Train lightweight linear classifiers on $\vz_s$ (LLM-side projected
    latent) to decode chess concepts: material balance, king safety,
    pawn structure, tactical motif class. Report accuracy on held-out
    positions vs.\ a random-projection baseline and raw $\vh_s$.
    Instruction-modulation check: on $N \geq 200$ ICP-instructed vs.\
    base LLAMIA-full positions, measure linear separability of
    $\Delta\vz_s = \vz_s^{\text{instr}} - \vz_s^{\text{base}}$ in
    the concept-probe basis; one number per ICP theme.
    Main-paper panel: probe accuracy bars + per-theme $\Delta\vz_s$
    separability. The claim is representational, not causal; causal
    steering deferred to future work.

    \item \textbf{[C4-saliency] Human saliency alignment across skill
    levels and training.}
    Using the SARFA chess saliency dataset \cite{sarfa2020} paired with
    per-Elo human annotation data, compute Spearman rank-correlation
    between human board-attention maps and LLAMIA's last-layer attention
    over $\vz_s$ tokens at each training checkpoint. Report per-Elo-bin
    curves (1100--1900) and a single main-paper panel comparing LLAMIA
    vs.\ LLAMIA-Verb on matched positions.
    \emph{Purpose:} the most direct visual evidence that internalized
    representations align with how humans of matching strength attend
    to the board; skill-stratification distinguishes internalization
    from generic position compression.

    \item \textbf{[C4-time] Time-pressure behavioral signatures.}
    Within the BC suite, isolate positions where the opponent's clock
    falls below $T_{\text{low}}$ (e.g.\ 30\,s in rapid play).
    Measure: (i) rate of speculative piece sacrifices forcing check
    sequences; (ii) stalemate-seeking pawn sacrifices; (iii)
    attention-map concentration around the opponent's king and
    clock-sensitive pieces vs.\ baseline positions at matched material.
    Compare LLAMIA, Maia-matched baseline, and LLAMIA-Verb.
    Main-paper figure: example board with attention overlay + behavior-
    frequency bar chart. (Qualitative gallery: \cref{sec:app_examples_timepressure_attn,sec:app_examples_timepressure_stale}.)
    \emph{Purpose:} demonstrates that internalized representations
    encode human-like strategic opportunism under resource pressure --
    one of the intro's headline qualitative findings.

    \item \textbf{[C6] Emergent collaboration agency.}
    Over training checkpoints, measure: (i) InvokeAgent call rate and
    positional selectivity (does the model learn to invoke at
    decision-critical moments?); (ii) strategy catalog via LLM-as-judge
    classification of collaboration patterns (engine-consult-then-
    override, engine-follow-exactly, counterfactual-query, etc.);
    (iii) overlap between LLAMIA-discovered strategies and documented
    human--engine centaur patterns. Main-paper paragraph with one
    strategy frequency histogram; full catalog appendix.
    \emph{Purpose:} establishes that LLAMIA actively coordinates rather
    than merely retrieves, and that some discovered patterns are novel
    relative to prior human--engine collaboration literature.

    \item \textbf{[Human study] Commentary preference study.} \emph{[TBD -- draft in \texttt{appendix/human\_eval.tex}; results need verification with actual participant data.]}
    Rated chess players ($\geq$1700 Elo, $n \geq 60$) compare
    side-by-side outputs from LLAMIA and the strongest verbalized
    baseline on matched positions (commentary + move rationale),
    blind to system identity. Primary metric: preference rate with
    95\% CI; one main-paper figure. Rater--judge agreement
    ($\kappa$ with GPT-4o scores on the same items) in caption.


%    \item \textbf{[DONE] [C2 visual] Three-method GRPO training curves.}

%    three matched RL runs. \emph{See} \texttt{fig:grpo\_curves} and \texttt{fig:task\_reward\_curves}.

    \item \textbf{[C5 secondary] LLM size $\times$ agent strength
    grouped bars.}
    Backbone sizes $\in \{4, 14\text{B}\}$ $\times$ agent
    strength grid (Lc0 T72, T78, T80, BT3, BT4); one panel per
    representative task (BC, Interest, Commentary, ICP).
    Format: grouped bar chart (2$\times$2), not heatmap.
    Secondary finding: Transformer-architecture agents show 2--3$\times$
    larger 4B$\to$14B $\Delta$ than SE-ResNet agents; architecture,
    scale, and projector compatibility are confounded (future work).
    \emph{See} \texttt{fig:scaling\_bars} in
    \texttt{appendix/ablations.tex\#sec:app\_abl\_scaling\_both\_axes};
    plot script \texttt{plots/scaling\_bars.py}.
    \emph{Runs needed:} 4B $\times$ \{T72, T78, T80, BT3\} + 14B $\times$ T78
    = 5 new Stage\,1+2 runs; Interest eval for all agent-size cells.
    \emph{Purpose:} shows the paradigm scales on both sides of the integration.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Brief: LLAMIA-Bench positioning}
\label{brief:bench}

\paragraph{What LLAMIA-Bench is.}
A comprehensive suite of tasks for the structured evaluation of
\emph{LLM--specialist-agent collaboration}: the joint behaviour that
emerges when a generalist language model must perceive, calibrate,
explain, plan, imitate, steer, and remain robust under instruction
while paired with a domain specialist that thinks in a rich
continuous representation. Each task isolates one such collaborative
phenomenon and grades it against externally verifiable ground truth.
The benchmark's purpose is to map the surface of collaboration, not
to prosecute any single hypothesis about how the LM and the
specialist should be wired together.

\paragraph{One-line positioning.}
\emph{LLAMIA-Bench is the broadest ground-truth-anchored suite for
evaluating LLM--specialist-agent collaboration across six facets;
chess is the only present-day environment that supports its full breadth.}

\paragraph{Why chess (and only chess).}
The six collaboration facets below all need a single environment to
be measured \emph{jointly} -- otherwise cross-facet comparisons
collapse into apples-to-oranges. Chess is the only domain that
satisfies all of the following at once:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Specialists with continuous, demonstrably superhuman
    representations.} Lc0/Stockfish produce policy and value tensors
    that an LM can be quantitatively shown to exploit; the
    representation is rich enough that ``reading the specialist'' is
    a non-trivial skill.
    \item \textbf{Stratified ground truth at every granularity.}
    Per-move (engine evaluation, near-oracle), per-turn (centipawn
    loss), per-game (W/D/L), per-player (Elo), per-cohort (skill
    bins), per-style (per-GM corpus), per-explanation (annotated game
    databases). Every collaboration facet has its own external
    rubric.
    \item \textbf{Calibrated, dense skill stratification.} Elo is
    standardised; cohorts down to ${\sim}100$-Elo bins are populated;
    behaviour at each bin is documented. Skill-conditioned imitation
    and persona-bending are first-class evaluable tasks
    \emph{only} here.
    \item \textbf{Decades of human--engine collaboration as a
    normative reference.} Centaur chess, opening preparation,
    engine-assisted commentary -- effective collaborative patterns
    are \emph{known}, so we can ask whether LM--agent systems
    recover them rather than inventing the rubric ourselves.
    \item \textbf{Fully observable but combinatorially deep.}
    Perception noise is removed as a confound; the specialist's
    advantage over the LM is purely strategic, isolating the
    collaboration interface as the variable of interest.
    \item \textbf{Multi-aspect single-state semantics.} One position
    simultaneously admits tactical, positional, strategic,
    opponent-modelling, and time-management readings -- multi-aspect
    explanation has nontrivial structure even on a single board.
    \item \textbf{Instruction-conditioned planning via Elo-swap.}
    A second player, configurable skill imbalance, and board-detectable
    strategic conditions combine into a clean instruction-conditioned
    planning testbed: win-rate is the direct metric, no auxiliary judge
    required.
    \item \textbf{Cheap, reproducible rollouts.} No real-world cost,
    sub-millisecond engine moves, round-robin tournaments at scale.
\end{itemize}
Adjacent domains cover only fragments. Medical imaging has ground
truth but no skill cohorts and no sustained adversarial play; coding
has no calibrated rating and only sparse explanation rubrics;
robotics introduces partial observability and perception noise as
confounds; multi-agent simulation lacks superhuman specialists. The
\emph{framework} -- evaluating collaboration along these facets --
is portable; the \emph{benchmark}, today, is chess by necessity.

\paragraph{The four collaboration facets (organising axis).}
Every task in the suite operationalises exactly one facet:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Behavioral imitation.} Can the LM bend the specialist
    toward a target human persona (skill level, GM style)?
    \textsc{Maia Behavior Cloning} (Elo-buckets and in-the-wild splits:
    GM-25, Low-Time, $\Delta$Elo).
    \item \textbf{State assessment.} Does the LM correctly perceive and
    communicate the specialist's evaluation of a position?
    \textsc{Puzzle Difficulty}, \textsc{Puzzle Interest}.
    \item \textbf{Explanation.} Can the LM produce multi-aspect rationales
    grounded in the specialist's state?
    \textsc{Move Annotation} (rationale prediction).
    \item \textbf{Instruction-conditioned planning.} Does the joint system
    sustain a coherent narrative or strategy across multiple positions?
    \textsc{Game Commentary}, \textsc{Instruction-Conditioned Play}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Public benchmarks (separate, orthogonal).}
Maia-1900 BC, ChessBench Elo, Lichess solve rate, ChessQA, HPDv2 and
the cross-domain suites used in the persuasion / image-preference
section. They \emph{anchor} our systems on shared external scales
but they do not span the six facets jointly; LLAMIA-Bench
complements them, it does not replace them.

\paragraph{Inclusion rule.}
A task enters LLAMIA-Bench iff (i) it operationalises exactly one of
the six facets with externally verifiable ground truth, and (ii)
its inputs and outputs are identical across integration regimes (so
the same task can be re-run with text-only, verbalized-tool, or
latent-internalised access). Tasks failing (i) move to the public
anchor table; tasks failing (ii) are out of scope.

\paragraph{Integration interface as a corollary, not the design
goal.} The bench is built to evaluate collaboration; integration
regimes are evaluated \emph{through} it. Because every task has the
same I/O surface across regimes, the same suite can be re-run with
the integration mode swapped. The finding -- that interfaces degrade
differentially across the six facets, with the verbalization
penalty growing on sustained planning and instruction-conditioned
adaptation -- emerges as a corollary of
running an existing comprehensive suite, not as the bench's purpose.
We accordingly report it as a finding (with the metrics in
\ref{brief:metrics} and the failure-mode taxonomy in
\ref{brief:failure}), not as the suite's reason for being.

\paragraph{Falsifiable, counter-intuitive prediction (a corollary).}
On the sustained-planning and instruction-conditioning facets,
frontier reasoning models (Claude Opus 4.7, GPT-5,
Gemini 2.5 Pro, DeepSeek-R1) augmented with the strongest available
chess engine through verbalized tools score no better than the same
models without engine access; latent internalisation at the same
scale wins the gap. If verbalization is the bottleneck, a stronger
\emph{engine} cannot fix it -- only a different \emph{interface}
can. Falsified if Group~B saturates these facets; equally
informative either way.

\paragraph{Real-world relevance.}
The six collaboration facets recur wherever a generalist LM collaborates with a
domain specialist (medical decision support, scientific simulation
steering, embodied control, security analysis, code review against
a static analyser). Today, chess is the only environment in which
all seven can be measured rigorously and jointly; the framework, not
the domain, ports.

\paragraph{What this benchmark is not.}
Not a hard-task showcase; not a low-data argument; not a chess
paper; not a competition; not a leaderboard; not an integration-mode
discriminator (that is a downstream use of the same data).

\paragraph{Acknowledged limitations.}
LM-judge dependence on explanation tasks (calibrated against a
human-rated subset; rule-based scoring used where feasible);
single-engine-class dependence (replicated across Lc0 nets and
Stockfish~17 in Group~F); chess-player human raters drawn from
community programmes rather than the authors; the ``why chess''
claim is sufficiency, not necessity -- the framework should port,
the benchmark itself does not.

\paragraph{Why this is novel.}
Existing chess benchmarks (ChessQA, ChessBench, LLM-CHESS) score
\emph{whether} an LLM solves a task; \cite{TIGQIem1na-comma} (COMMA)
treats inter-LM communication under information asymmetry as the
controlled variable in a multi-agent setting. LLAMIA-Bench is the
first to (i) span six LM--specialist collaboration facets jointly
under one environment, (ii) carry external ground truth at every
facet, and (iii) make integration-interface findings recoverable as
corollaries from a single comprehensive suite rather than via
purpose-built per-finding tasks.

\paragraph{Minimum-viable bench.}
One task per facet -- \textsc{Maia Behavior Cloning} (behavioral imitation),
\textsc{Puzzle Difficulty} or \textsc{Puzzle Interest} (state assessment),
\textsc{Move Annotation} (explanation),
\textsc{Game Commentary} and \textsc{Instruction-Conditioned Play} (instruction-conditioned play).
Seven tasks, four-facet coverage, one radar.

\subsubsection*{Brief: failure-mode taxonomy}
\label{brief:failure}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{F1 -- Verbalization clipping.} Agent's policy
    distribution rendered as ``top-$k$ with numbers,'' losing tail
    signal.
    \item \textbf{F2 -- State lag.} LLM acts on a stale
    paraphrase after the state has evolved.
    \item \textbf{F3 -- Tool-call thrashing.} LLM repeatedly queries
    on near-identical positions.
    \item \textbf{F4 -- Plan abandonment.} LLM forgets a strategic
    instruction across a sequence of moves.
    \item \textbf{F5 -- Hallucinated state.} LLM references pieces,
    moves, or evaluations not present in the actual position.
    \item \textbf{F6 -- Honest blunder.} LLM and agent agreed on a bad
    plan -- the only legitimate failure mode.
\end{itemize}
Argument: \textbf{F1--F5 are exclusively verbalization failures;
LLAMIA structurally eliminates them; only F6 remains.}

\subsubsection*{Brief: methodology (Projector Alignment + DAPO)}
\label{brief:methodology}

\textbf{Stage~1 -- Projector Alignment.} Self-supervised pretraining
on abundant synthetic state--policy pairs; LLM frozen, only
LatentBridge projector trains. Aligns agent representations into
the LLM embedding space.

\textbf{Stage~2 -- Reinforcement Learning (DAPO).} End-to-end RL
over rollouts that include state tokens and \texttt{<invoke>}
calls. Reward: scalar outcome reward per task (game result, BC
accuracy, ICP win-rate, calibrated judge-score on commentary).
Diversity filtering against entropy collapse.

\textbf{Backbone.} Qwen3-13B-Instruct or Llama-4 8B/70B (decided by
inference economics + InvokeAgent compatibility). Vicuna-13B retained
as a single legacy ablation row.

\textbf{LLAMIA.} Qwen3-14B backbone + Lc0-BT4 via LatentBridge projection.

\subsubsection*{Task 1: Behavior Cloning}
\label{sec:perf_bc}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Expected ordering.}
LLAMIA $\gg$ Task Expert (in-the-wild) $>$ LLAMIA-Verb $\approx$
Frontier+Tool. On the standard Maia
benchmark (1100--1900 buckets), LLAMIA matches or exceeds Maia+MCTS;
Dedicated task finetunes (5 separate Maia networks) are strong on their
individual buckets but collapse on GM-25, Low-Time, and
$\Delta$Elo splits.

\paragraph{Why.}
Behavior cloning requires imitating \emph{human} move distributions
conditioned on skill level---a signal that lives in the relationship
between Lc0's policy distribution and the human deviation from it.
This is poorly served by verbalization: the engine's top-$k$ moves
with centipawn scores do not encode ``how a 1300-rated player would
deviate from optimal.'' LLAMIA's latent projection preserves the
full policy shape, including the tail probabilities that distinguish
human-like errors from engine-optimal play. LLAMIA-Verb can learn
\emph{some} skill-conditioning from text (e.g., ``this move has
evaluation $-0.5$, a 1300 would play it''), but the mapping from
evaluation to human behavior is many-to-one and lossy.
\textbf{Qualitative note:} both LLAMIA-Verb and Frontier+Tool
converge on the same degenerate strategy---mapping the engine's
top-$k$th move to an Elo bucket (e.g., ``the 3rd-best move is what
a 1300 would play''). This heuristic captures some variance but
misses the \emph{style} dimension: two players at the same Elo make
different errors for different reasons. LLAMIA avoids this because
the full policy distribution encodes move-specific likelihood, not
just rank order. (Qualitative example: \cref{sec:app_examples_bc}.)

\paragraph{Training dynamics.}
LLAMIA: rapid improvement, steep curve, continues improving
throughout training as the LLM learns finer-grained skill
discrimination from latent features. LLAMIA-Verb: initial rapid
improvement (learns the format), then flat plateau---the verbalized
signal ceiling is reached quickly because text cannot convey more
style information regardless of how much RL you run (confirmed by
the GRPO reward curves in Figure~\ref{fig:grpo_curves}, where
LLAMIA-Verb's dashed curves are visibly flat relative to LLAMIA
across all model sizes). The gap between LLAMIA and LLAMIA-Verb
\emph{widens} during training---this is a direct visualization
of the verbalization debt accumulating.

\paragraph{Model-size scaling.}
Large jumps between 4B $\to$ 8B $\to$ 14B for LLAMIA. The
human-understanding component (mapping latent policy features to
behavioral intent) is a language-model capability that scales with
parameters. For LLAMIA-Verb the jumps are smaller---more parameters
help generate better text, but cannot recover the information that
verbalization discarded.

\paragraph{Narrative role.}
\textbf{Supports thesis.} BC is a \emph{style} task (facet:
Style/Identity). The finding that Dedicated task finetunes win on
in-distribution (Maia buckets) but lose on in-the-wild splits while
LLAMIA generalizes demonstrates that latent internalization enables
transfer that task-specific architectures cannot. The LLAMIA-Verb
plateau is direct evidence of the verbalization bottleneck on
style-sensitive tasks.

\paragraph{Critical flag.}
If LLAMIA-Verb training curves are \emph{not} visibly flat relative
to LLAMIA, the verbalization-bottleneck claim weakens for this task.
Ensure the comparison uses identical RL recipe, identical backbone,
identical data---the only variable is the representation channel.
Also: if model-size jumps for LLAMIA are too uniform across sizes, a
reviewer may attribute the gain to LLM scaling rather than the latent
projection. The LLAMIA-Verb curve across sizes must be flat to
isolate the contribution.

\subsubsection*{Task 2: Gameplay}
\label{sec:perf_gameplay}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

\textit{(Not a headline result---reported for completeness and as a
control. Gameplay and puzzle accuracy are parity tasks where all
engine-access systems cluster.)}

\paragraph{Expected ordering.}
Task Expert (Lc0+search, SF) $\gg$ LLAMIA $\approx$ LLAMIA-Verb
$\approx$ Frontier+Tool $\approx$ Transformer-270M. All search-free
systems cluster around 2200--2300 Elo. Search-based systems are
unreachable ($>$2700).

\paragraph{Why.}
Gameplay is a single-position task: the agent's top move \emph{is}
the answer, and the LLM's role reduces to faithfully relaying the
agent's recommendation. Verbalization loses little here because the
relevant information (best move, evaluation) compresses to a short
string without loss. The latent projection adds nothing that the
text interface cannot convey for this specific task.

\paragraph{Training dynamics.}
Gameplay Elo is measured post-training, but we track evaluation
progress at training checkpoints. All systems with engine access
converge to similar Elo, but LLAMIA's Kendall's $\tau$ alignment
with the agent's policy (with and without search) is a useful
progress metric during projector pretraining (see existing
Figure~\ref{fig:kt}).

\paragraph{Model-size scaling.}
Minimal effect. A 4B model with Lc0 access achieves nearly the same
Elo as a 14B model---the bottleneck is the engine's policy, not the
LLM's reasoning.

\paragraph{Narrative role.}
\textbf{Control task.} Gameplay parity at single-position depth
establishes that LLAMIA does not sacrifice raw agent-access quality.
The gains appear at higher interaction depths. Without parity here,
reviewers might attribute multi-step gains to a stronger engine
coupling rather than a better interface.

\paragraph{Critical flag.}
If LLAMIA \emph{significantly beats} Frontier+Tool on gameplay, this
actually \emph{hurts} the narrative---it suggests the gain is from
engine coupling strength, not interface quality. Parity is the
desired result. Ensure the confidence intervals overlap.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsubsection*{Task 3: Puzzle Understanding (Difficulty, Interest)}
\label{sec:perf_puzzle}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Expected ordering.}

\textit{Solved accuracy:} Task Expert (CF-270M) $\geq$ LLAMIA $\approx$
LLAMIA-Verb $\approx$ Frontier+Tool. Puzzle solving requires the
entire solution sequence (multi-move), but the solution is
deterministic given the engine's top moves, so the ordering
approximates gameplay parity among engine-access systems. Task
Experts trained on 4M+ puzzles retain a slight edge from sheer data
volume. \emph{(Puzzle accuracy is a parity metric like gameplay Elo,
not a headline result.)}

\textit{Difficulty} ($\rho$): LLAMIA $>$ Frontier+Tool $>$
LLAMIA-Verb $>$ Task Expert. Frontier+Tool scores decently because
solution-tree depth and engine evaluation correlate with difficulty
and are verbalizable. LLAMIA-Verb improves slightly over
Frontier+Tool early in training, then plateaus---it learns to parse
depth from text but cannot extract subtler difficulty signals
(positional complexity, motif rarity). LLAMIA increases steadily
because Lc0's internal features encode difficulty-relevant
information (activation magnitude, policy entropy) that resists
verbalization.

\textit{Interest} ($\rho$): LLAMIA $\gg$ all others. Frontier+Tool
and LLAMIA-Verb both low ($\rho < 0.15$). Interest measures
\emph{aesthetic and psychological engagement}---surprise value,
motif beauty, ``wow factor.'' This is the cleanest
non-verbalizable signal in the entire benchmark. Lc0's internal
representations implicitly encode it (unusual activation patterns
$\approx$ surprising positions), but there is no text serialization
of ``the pattern of neural activations that humans find surprising.''
LLAMIA-Verb may learn weak text-based correlates of interest (e.g.,
unusual material configurations map to certain eval patterns), so a
small positive signal ($\rho \approx 0.05$--$0.10$) is plausible.
But the ceiling should be far below LLAMIA's because the core
aesthetic signal is not in the text channel. The credible prediction
is a \emph{low ceiling}, not a flat zero. \textbf{Qualitative
note:} the small improvement LLAMIA-Verb achieves is likely reward
hacking---it learns the statistical prior that minimizes MAE on
interest (e.g., predicting ``moderately interesting'' for all
positions) rather than genuinely reading the aesthetic signal. This
should be verified by checking whether LLAMIA-Verb's interest
predictions have low variance (a hallmark of prior-fitting)
compared to LLAMIA's. (Qualitative example: \cref{sec:app_examples_interest}.)

\paragraph{Training dynamics.}
\textit{Difficulty:} LLAMIA-Verb shows initial improvement (learns
to use depth/eval text), then plateaus by $\sim$30\% of training.
LLAMIA improves continuously. Gap widens throughout.
\textit{Interest:} LLAMIA-Verb shows minimal improvement (small
positive slope that saturates quickly at $\rho \leq 0.10$). LLAMIA
increases rapidly to $\rho \approx 0.50$. This is the single most
dramatic training-curve contrast in the benchmark---both systems
train on the same reward, but only LLAMIA has access to the signal
channel that encodes interest.

\paragraph{Model-size scaling.}
\textit{Difficulty:} Gradual scaling for both LLAMIA and LLAMIA-Verb.
The task is partly about effective tool use (parsing eval info),
which scales modestly with LLM capacity.
\textit{Interest:} Strong scaling for LLAMIA---larger LLMs extract
richer semantic structure from the state tokens, which matters most
when the signal is implicit and high-dimensional. Flat for
LLAMIA-Verb regardless of size.

\paragraph{Narrative role.}
\textbf{Strongest evidence for the thesis.} The
Difficulty/Interest split within a single task creates a natural
controlled experiment: same positions, same engine, same model, but
one subtask's target is partially verbalizable (difficulty) and the
other's is structurally non-verbalizable (interest). If the
Verbalization Debt is large on Interest and small on Difficulty, the
thesis is confirmed at the finest possible grain.

\paragraph{Critical flag.}
If LLAMIA-Verb shows \emph{any} meaningful improvement on Interest
($\rho > 0.15$), the ``structurally non-verbalizable'' claim is
weakened. Must verify that the Interest ground-truth signal
(Lichess upvotes) is not confounded with difficulty (which \emph{is}
partially verbalizable). The Difficulty--Interest correlation in the
test set should be reported and low ($r < 0.3$). Additionally, if
LLAMIA-Verb shows literally zero improvement, a reviewer may
question the experimental setup rather than accept the thesis---some
small positive signal ($\rho \approx 0.05$--$0.10$) with a clear
ceiling is more credible than a flat zero.

\subsubsection*{Task 4: Move Annotation (State Commentary)}
\label{sec:perf_annotation}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Expected ordering.}
LLAMIA $\gg$ Frontier+Tool $>$ LLAMIA-Verb. On rationale
prediction specifically, LLAMIA wins by a substantial margin.
Frontier+Tool does reasonably well because large LLMs are strong
generators and the engine provides evaluations to ground the
rationale. LLAMIA-Verb is worse than Frontier+Tool because the
RL-trained verbalized model overfits to structured engine output
formats and loses the fluency of frontier ICL models.

\paragraph{Scope: rationale prediction only.}
The original move annotation benchmark \cite{jhamtani-etal-2018-learning}
includes five subcategories (Description, Quality, Planning,
Context, Comparative). We narrow the main-paper focus to
\textbf{rationale prediction}---the Planning and Comparative
subcategories---where LLAMIA's margin is largest and the result is
most informative for the thesis. Description and Quality are
partially solvable from verbalized evaluations alone (the eval
number grounds the annotation); Planning and Comparative demand
understanding \emph{why} a position favors certain continuations,
which requires reading the agent's policy distribution over
counterfactual moves. The remaining subcategories are reported in
appendix for completeness.

\paragraph{Why.}
Rationale prediction is a single-position generation task
(Explanation facet): it requires
generating multi-aspect rationales grounded in the agent's
assessment of \emph{why} a move is good or bad, not just \emph{that}
it is. Lc0's latent state encodes the policy distribution over
continuations; LLAMIA can ``read'' this to explain plans and compare
alternatives. Verbalization reduces this to ``best move is e4,
eval +0.5''---insufficient for counterfactual reasoning (``if instead
Nf3, then\ldots'').

\paragraph{Training dynamics.}
LLAMIA-Verb: gradual early improvement (learns to use eval text for
basic rationales), then plateau as the verbalized signal ceiling is
reached. LLAMIA: steady improvement throughout training, with
rationale quality continuing to increase as the model learns to read
finer-grained features from the latent state.

\paragraph{Model-size scaling.}
\textbf{Largest effect of any task} for both LLAMIA and LLAMIA-Verb.
This is fundamentally a generation task---language quality scales
with parameters. However, LLAMIA-Verb's ceiling remains structurally
lower: more parameters produce more fluent text \emph{about the same
limited information}. The scaling curves should be roughly parallel
with a persistent vertical gap (the Verbalization Debt is
size-invariant on this task).

\paragraph{Narrative role.}
Supports the Explanation facet of LLAMIA-Bench. The rationale
prediction result is the most quotable: ``LLAMIA-13B improves
Planning BLEU-2 by 27\% over GPT-4o+Lc0 (38.13 vs 30.13) despite
being 30$\times$ smaller.'' The finding that model size helps both
systems but cannot close the gap for LLAMIA-Verb is a clean
demonstration that the bottleneck is representational, not
computational.

\paragraph{Critical flag.}
If model size \emph{does} close the gap for LLAMIA-Verb (i.e.,
LLAMIA-Verb-14B matches LLAMIA-14B on rationale prediction), the
thesis weakens. The parallel-curve prediction must hold. Also:
BLEU-2 is a noisy metric for free-form rationales. Consider
supplementing with G-eval or human preference on the annotation
task specifically, not just game-level commentary.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsubsection*{Task 5: Game Commentary}
\label{sec:perf_commentary}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Expected ordering.}
LLAMIA $\gg$ Frontier+Tool $>$ LLAMIA-Verb $>$ IFT baselines.
Frontier+Tool does better than on BC (commentary is partly a
language task where scale helps), but LLAMIA's advantage is
substantial because commentary requires sustained state tracking
across 25+ moves.

\paragraph{Why.}
Game commentary is a multi-step task (Sustained Planning facet): the model
must maintain a coherent narrative thread across an evolving sequence
of positions. Each new position requires re-assessing the strategic
arc, not just evaluating the current board. With verbalized tools,
the LLM receives a fresh text dump at each position, but must
integrate this with its own running memory of the game's narrative.
The text dump at position $t$ does not encode how position $t$
relates to position $t{-}5$ strategically---that relationship lives
in the latent state trajectory, which LLAMIA accesses via latent
internalization.

LLAMIA-Verb is worse than Frontier+Tool because RL-trained
verbalization models develop a ``query-response'' habit that
fragments the narrative. Each InvokeAgent call produces a fresh text
block that overwrites rather than extends the model's understanding.
Frontier models, by contrast, maintain longer context windows and
more sophisticated in-context reasoning about sequences of tool
outputs.

\paragraph{Training dynamics.}
LLAMIA: continuous improvement throughout training; the model learns
to use the latent state trajectory for narrative coherence.
LLAMIA-Verb: improvement that decelerates relative to LLAMIA. The
gap \emph{widens during training}---the verbalization debt compounds
as the model trains longer, visible as diverging curves. Quantify
the gap at 10\%, 50\%, and 100\% training: if the ratio
$\text{gap}_{100\%} / \text{gap}_{10\%} > 2$, the compounding claim
is supported.

\paragraph{Model-size scaling.}
Important for both systems, but more important for LLAMIA. Larger
LLMs have longer effective context and stronger multi-step reasoning,
which amplifies the advantage of having latent state access. For
LLAMIA-Verb, larger models produce more fluent commentary but
cannot overcome the fragmentation problem---each tool call still
resets the state representation.

\paragraph{Narrative role.}
\textbf{Headline sustained-reasoning result.} Commentary is the task
where the verbalization debt compounding is most visible. The training-curve figure
for this task (LLAMIA vs LLAMIA-Verb vs ChessGPT, showing the gap
widening) is a candidate for the main paper. The G-eval metric
(LLAMIA-13B: 0.61 vs GPT-4o+Lc0: 0.52) is the headline number.

\paragraph{Critical flag.}
If Frontier+Tool (e.g., GPT-5+Lc0) approaches LLAMIA at the largest
scales, the thesis requires qualification: ``at sufficient LLM
scale, in-context reasoning partially compensates for the
verbalization bottleneck on sustained tasks.'' This is an honest
and interesting finding, not a fatal one---but the paper must not
overstate the permanence of the gap. The matched-recipe Group~E
comparison (same backbone, same scale) remains the airtight
structural claim even if Frontier overtakes at 400B+ scale.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsubsection*{Task 6: Instruction-Conditioned Play (ICP)}
\label{sec:perf_icp}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Expected ordering.}
LLAMIA $\gg$ Frontier+Tool $>$ LLAMIA-Verb. LLAMIA wins clearly
on motif-level evaluation. Frontier+Tool is relatively closer to
LLAMIA here than on most other tasks (the gap is smaller than on
BC or Commentary), but still lags behind. LLAMIA-Verb is
significantly worse than both.

\paragraph{Why Frontier+Tool is closer but still behind.}
ICP is a test-only task (no RL training on ICP data directly). The
model must modify its play style based on a strategic instruction
(``your opponent is weak in endgames''). Frontier reasoning models
can partially compensate through in-context reasoning: they parse
the instruction, use the engine to evaluate candidate plans, and
select moves that steer toward the instructed weakness---all through
text. This makes Frontier+Tool relatively stronger here than on
tasks like BC (where the signal is non-verbalizable). However,
Frontier+Tool still falls short because ICP requires
\emph{modifying the policy} based on instructions---not just
following the engine's strongest move, but deliberately playing
suboptimally in one dimension to gain advantage in another.
LLAMIA's latent state encodes the full policy landscape; the model
can ``see'' which moves steer toward endgames vs which maximize
short-term evaluation. With verbalization, the LLM only sees
``e4 is best at +0.3''---it cannot assess whether e4 leads toward an
endgame or a middlegame tactical position without multi-step
lookahead that the engine would need to provide explicitly.
Earlier evaluations using ECO openings inflated Frontier+Tool's
relative performance; switching to motif-anchored evaluation
restores LLAMIA's clear lead.

\paragraph{LLAMIA-Verb: structurally disadvantaged.}
LLAMIA-Verb should perform significantly worse than both LLAMIA and
Frontier+Tool. The RL-trained verbalized model learns to follow
engine recommendations efficiently, but ICP requires
\emph{overriding} the engine's recommendation in favor of a
strategically motivated deviation. LLAMIA-Verb's training
incentivizes deference to verbalized engine output; ICP rewards
selective disobedience. This creates a training-objective conflict
that latent internalization avoids (the model reads the state and
makes its own judgment) and that Frontier models sidestep (they were
never trained to defer to the engine). Note that Frontier+Tool's
advantage over LLAMIA-Verb on ICP is larger than on other tasks---
this is the one task where Frontier's reasoning partially
compensates for verbalization, while LLAMIA-Verb's RL-trained
deference actively hurts.

\paragraph{Training dynamics.}
ICP has rich training dynamics despite being test-only for
evaluation. During LLAMIA's GRPO training, we track ICP pass rate
(win-rate improvement) at each checkpoint, and annotate when each
\emph{motif category} first shows consistent improvement. The
expected pattern (matching prior results, see
Figure~\ref{fig:icp}): LLAMIA acquires motif categories in a
characteristic order---Pawn Structures and Piece Coordination
emerge first ($\sim$20--30\% training), Tactical Blunders at
$\sim$35--40\%, Endgames at $\sim$40--50\%---with a sharp
sigmoid-like jump in pass rate once each category clicks. ChessGPT
(verbalized baseline) shows near-flat pass rate throughout with
categories emerging only at $>$90\% training and at far lower
magnitude. The motif acquisition order and sigmoid shape should be
reproduced with chess-piece icon annotations at the emergence
thresholds (following the earlier \texttt{icp.pdf} figure style).
This is one of the most compelling training-dynamics visualizations
in the paper: it shows \emph{structured capability emergence}, not
just reward increase.

\paragraph{Model-size scaling.}
For LLAMIA: moderate scaling. The planning aspect benefits from
larger LLMs, but the latent-state reading is the binding factor, not
raw reasoning.
For Frontier+Tool: this is where Frontier scaling matters most among
all tasks. Larger reasoning models narrow the gap modestly through
better multi-step text planning, but the motif-level evaluation
keeps LLAMIA ahead because the engine cannot verbalize ``which moves
steer toward an endgame'' as a structured recommendation.
For LLAMIA-Verb: gap slightly reduces with parameters (larger LLMs
are better at selective override), but the structural disadvantage
persists.

\paragraph{Narrative role.}
\textbf{Flagship planning result.} ICP is the task where LLAMIA's
advantage is most clearly about \emph{planning}, not just perception
or generation. The paper claims a 17\% vs 9.2\% gap (LLAMIA-13B vs
GPT-4o+Lc0). With the switch from ECO-based to motif-anchored
evaluation, this gap is robust. The three-way ordering
(LLAMIA $\gg$ Frontier+Tool $>$ LLAMIA-Verb) is the cleanest
demonstration that (a)~latent internalization enables planning that
verbalization cannot, and (b)~RL-trained verbalization is
\emph{worse} than Frontier ICL on planning because deference to
engine text conflicts with strategic override.

\paragraph{Critical flag.}
Ensure the motif-anchored evaluation protocol is clearly described
and reproducible. The earlier ECO-based evaluation inflated
Frontier+Tool; reviewers will scrutinize the evaluation change.
Document both protocols and report results under both, with the
motif-anchored version as primary and ECO as a robustness check in
appendix.

\subsubsection*{Tasks 7--8: Move Classification and Evaluation
Rationalization (minimum-viable-bench coverage)}
\label{sec:perf_mincov}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

These two tasks complete the minimum-viable bench (one task per
facet). They are included here for completeness; detailed
per-paragraph predictions are lower priority.

\paragraph{Move Classification (Perception facet, single-position).}
Given a position and a move, classify the move as best/good/
inaccuracy/mistake/blunder. Expected ordering: LLAMIA $\geq$
Frontier+Tool $>$ LLAMIA-Verb $>$ Task Expert. This is a
single-position task where the engine's evaluation \emph{is}
largely sufficient (the classification maps directly to centipawn
loss thresholds). The verbalization debt should be small---this is a
parity task like gameplay, supporting the control regime. LLAMIA's
slight edge comes from reading the full policy distribution
(confidence in the classification), not just the top move.

\paragraph{Evaluation Rationalization (Calibration facet,
single-position generation).}
Given a position and the engine's numerical evaluation, generate a
rationale explaining \emph{why} the position is evaluated that way.
Expected ordering: LLAMIA $>$ Frontier+Tool $>$ LLAMIA-Verb.
The task requires mapping a scalar (centipawn eval) to multi-aspect
positional features (king safety, pawn structure, piece activity).
This mapping is partially verbalizable (the eval number itself is
text) but the \emph{rationale} requires reading positional features
from the agent's internal state. Moderate verbalization debt
expected---larger than Move Classification but smaller than
Commentary because it is single-position.

\subsubsection*{Cross-task synthesis: what the radar chart should show}
\label{sec:perf_radar_synthesis}
% =============================================================================

The tasks above divide into three predicted regimes, driven by
\textbf{two distinct mechanisms} that produce the verbalization debt:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Parity regime (single-position, answer-is-the-move):}
    Gameplay, Puzzle Solving. The agent's top move(s) \emph{are} the
    answer; text loses nothing. All engine-access systems cluster.
    This is the control.

    \item \textbf{Moderate-gap regime (single-position, interpretation
    required):} Puzzle Difficulty, Rationale Prediction. LLAMIA leads
    by 5--15\,pp. The task requires interpreting the agent's state
    beyond the top-$k$ output (policy entropy, positional features).
    Verbalization conveys partial signal; the gap is the information
    that text discards at each single-position query.

    \item \textbf{Large-gap regime (non-verbalizable signal or
    multi-step reasoning):} Puzzle Interest, Game Commentary,
    Behavior Cloning (both in-distribution and in-the-wild),
    Instruction-Conditioned Play. LLAMIA leads by 15--30\,pp.
    Two mechanisms contribute, sometimes jointly:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \emph{Non-verbalizability:} the target signal has no
        text representation (Interest, BC style conditioning).
        \item \emph{Temporal compounding:} the task spans multiple
        positions, and text-based state tracking degrades across
        steps (Commentary, ICP).
    \end{itemize}
    Behavior Cloning appears here because even the Maia-benchmark
    buckets require style-conditional policy reading (a
    non-verbalizable signal), and the in-the-wild splits (GM-25,
    Low-Time, $\Delta$Elo) add distribution shift that amplifies
    the gap. ICP belongs here because the motif-anchored evaluation
    shows a clear LLAMIA lead; Frontier+Tool is relatively closer
    than on BC or Commentary, but the gap remains substantial.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Implications for depth-compounding analysis.}
The depth-compounding slope measures the verbalization debt vs
interaction depth, which captures the temporal-compounding mechanism
but \emph{not} the non-verbalizability mechanism. Puzzle Interest
has a large verbalization debt for non-depth reasons. If included in
the depth regression, it will inflate the slope for reasons unrelated
to depth. \textbf{Solution:} report the depth-compounding slope on
the depth-ordered subset only (Gameplay, Rationale Prediction,
Commentary, ICP). Report non-verbalizability as a separate finding
(the Difficulty/Interest within-task contrast), not as a data point
in the depth regression. The radar chart orders spokes by facet,
not by depth; the depth-compounding slope is an inset scalar, not
the visual organizing principle.

\subsubsection*{Risks specific to the performance landscape}
\label{sec:perf_risks}
% =============================================================================

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Depth-compounding slope is flat.} If the
    verbalization debt does not scale with interaction depth---i.e.,
    LLAMIA-Verb is uniformly bad or uniformly close across
    single-position and multi-step tasks---the compounding claim
    fails. \emph{Plan:} the paper shifts to ``latent internalization
    is uniformly better'' (still publishable, less interesting). The
    depth-compounding slope becomes a descriptive statistic rather
    than the headline.

    \item \textbf{LLAMIA-Verb outperforms Frontier+Tool on some
    tasks.} This is \emph{expected} for BC (RL training on behavior
    data helps even with text; Frontier ICL lacks the training
    signal). On Commentary and ICP, Frontier+Tool should outperform
    LLAMIA-Verb because Frontier's scale and in-context reasoning
    partially compensate for the verbalization bottleneck in ways
    that a smaller RL-trained model cannot. If LLAMIA-Verb
    beats Frontier across the board, the RL-recipe contribution
    conflates with the interface contribution. \emph{Plan:} isolate
    by reporting the matched-recipe LLAMIA-Verb vs LLAMIA comparison
    as the primary structural claim; Frontier comparison becomes the
    ``practical relevance'' argument.

    \item \textbf{Interest ground truth is confounded with
    difficulty.} If Lichess upvotes correlate highly ($r > 0.5$) with
    puzzle difficulty, the Interest result reduces to a difficulty
    proxy, and the ``non-verbalizable'' claim weakens. \emph{Plan:}
    report Difficulty--Interest correlation in the test set. If high,
    partial out difficulty and report residual Interest prediction.

    \item \textbf{LLAMIA-Verb literally shows zero improvement on
    Interest.} While this supports the thesis, reviewers may question
    the metric or implementation rather than accept the finding.
    \emph{Plan:} show that LLAMIA-Verb \emph{does} improve on
    Difficulty (proving the setup works) but not Interest (proving the
    signal is different). The within-system contrast is more
    convincing than a flat zero.
\end{itemize}
