\begin{abstract}                    
  LLMs are increasingly deployed as orchestrators that coordinate specialized subagents
  to solve complex tasks. In many important domains (game playing, robotics, autonomous driving),
  the strongest available agents are not language models: \yaman{two independent sentences not connected very well} their expertise is encoded in latent
  representations of policy, value, and learned features, not in words. Integrating such \yaman{what is such pointing to?} agents
  into LLM reasoning pipelines currently requires \emph{verbalization}: compressing rich continuous
  representations into sparse textual summaries at each interaction step. To study whether verbalization
  constitutes a structural bottleneck \yaman{of what?}, we introduce \textsc{LLAMIA-Bench}, a suite of six collaborative chess tasks spanning three facets: behavioral imitation, state
  assessment, and natural-language explanation. Tasks are drawn from prominent problems in chess
  literature and industry, each unsolvable by either component alone \yaman{which componenets?}, and selected to vary in how much
  relevant signal survives verbalization. Our experiments reveal a consistent \emph{verbalization debt}:
  the performance gap between verbalized and internalized integration \yaman{where was internalized integration introduced?} widens throughout training \yaman{why did we suddenly bring up training?} and persists as the LLM scales from 4B to 14B parameters. The effect is sharpest on tasks whose target
  signal has no faithful text representation: on puzzle interest prediction, verbalized systems of all
  sizes remain near-chance (${\leq}12/100$) while internalization reaches $52/100$. To close this gap, we introduce \emph{latent state internalization}, which
  projects the subagent's continuous representations directly into the LLM's token stream as learned
  state tokens, with dynamic re-encoding as actions advance the environment state \yaman{break it into 2 sentences}. A single 14B model,
  \textsc{LLAMIA}, trained under this paradigm matches or exceeds dedicated task specialists and
  frontier models including GPT-5.1 with tool access across all benchmark tasks \yaman{why this baseline suddenly? this baseline represents collaboration via verbalization}, and generalizes
  out-of-distribution where task-specific finetunes collapse \yaman{why this baseline?}. In a human study with skilled chess players, LLAMIA passes as human \yaman{so what? what is the importance of this line?} in 61\% of gameplay trials and its commentary is preferred in 72.2\% of pairings. \yaman{so what?}
  \end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced latent state internalization, a method for LLM agent integration that replaces verbalized communication with direct projection of the agent's continuous representations into the LLM's embedding space. Through controlled experiments on \textsc{LLAMIA-Bench}---seven tasks spanning behavioral imitation, state assessment, explanation, and instruction-conditioned play---we established that verbalization acts as a structural bottleneck: the performance gap between internalized and verbalized integration widens with interaction depth and does not close by scaling the LLM or the RL budget within our evaluated range (up to 14B parameters).

A single LLAMIA-14B model, trained via projector alignment followed by DAPO, matches or exceeds dedicated task finetunes in aggregate, with the exception of Allie-Adaptive-Search on its in-distribution Elo buckets. The verbalization debt---the performance gap between internalized and verbalized integration---widens on tasks requiring sustained multi-step collaboration and on tasks whose target signal resists text serialization (e.g., puzzle interest), confirming that learned invocation timing and latent-state reading are both essential for effective LLM--agent integration.
