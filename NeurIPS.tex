 \begin{abstract}                                                                                          
  Multi-agent systems coordinate specialists to solve tasks that require both general reasoning and
  domain-specific expertise. Because such systems communicate in natural language, prior work has focused on
   LLM-to-LLM collaboration. But in many important domains such as game playing, robotics, autonomous
  driving, the strongest available agents are not language models; their expertise is encoded in latent    
  representations of policy, value, and learned features. Integrating them into LLM systems today relies on
  \emph{verbalization}: compressing rich continuous representations into sparse textual summaries. Whether
  verbalization is adequate for deep collaboration remains an open question. Evaluating multi-agent collaboration also requires domains that combine verifiable metrics with meaningful
   tasks. Chess uniquely provides both: humans have collaborated with engines for decades across commentary,
   analysis, and planning, producing well-defined tasks with established protocols. We curate               
  \textsc{LLAMIA-Bench}, a suite of collaborative chess tasks spanning behavior cloning, stylometry, puzzle
  understanding, commentary, and instruction-conditioned planning, and show that verbalized RL baselines
  fall short on tasks requiring deeper collaboration. To address this, we introduce \emph{latent state
  internalization}, an integration paradigm that projects the subagent's continuous representation into the
  LLM's chain of thought as latent tokens, with dynamic re-encoding as actions advance the environment. A
  single 13B model trained under this paradigm, \textsc{LLAMIA}, matches or exceeds task-specific
  specialists across every benchmark category.
  \end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced latent state internalization, a method for LLM agent integration that replaces verbalized communication with direct projection of the agent's continuous representations into the LLM's embedding space. Through controlled experiments on \textsc{LLAMIA-Bench}---seven tasks spanning behavioral imitation, state assessment, explanation, and instruction-conditioned play---we established that verbalization acts as a structural bottleneck: the performance gap between internalized and verbalized integration widens with interaction depth and does not close by scaling the LLM or the RL budget within our evaluated range (up to 14B parameters).

A single LLAMIA-14B model, trained via projector alignment followed by DAPO, matches or exceeds dedicated task finetunes in aggregate, with the exception of Allie-Adaptive-Search on its in-distribution Elo buckets. The verbalization debt---the performance gap between internalized and verbalized integration---widens on tasks requiring sustained multi-step collaboration and on tasks whose target signal resists text serialization (e.g., puzzle interest), confirming that learned invocation timing and latent-state reading are both essential for effective LLM--agent integration.

\paragraph{Future work.}
The framework requires three components: a subagent with extractable representations, a lightweight projector, and a differentiable reward signal for end-to-end RL. Robotics policies (RT-2, Octo), game-playing agents, and scientific simulation controllers satisfy these requirements and are natural next instantiations. Two open questions are whether internalization scales to multiple heterogeneous agents within a single reasoning trace, and whether the structured capability emergence observed in instruction-conditioned play (\cref{sec:results_skill_discovery}) replicates across domains.
